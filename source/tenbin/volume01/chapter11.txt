Episode 11: A Bouquet of Vegetable Flowers
Saturday morning, after breakfast.

Hayato was roped into Himeko’s fashion check in the living room.

“How’s this? Too childish?” Himeko twirled, her floral sleeveless dress flaring—a sweet, girly design. She hummed, dissatisfied.

“Uh, looks fine?” Hayato offered.

“Ugh, Onii! Be serious!” Himeko snapped.

This was her third outfit consultation. Before this, she tried a mature low-cut top with a midi tulle skirt, and before that, the age-appropriate outfit from the convenience store night.

(They all look good. Any would do.)

Himeko owned a sizable wardrobe with specific preferences, but Hayato couldn’t grasp her obsession and felt stumped. His clueless expression only puffed Himeko’s cheeks further.

As she moved to scold him, the clock caught her eye, sparking panic.

“Oh, I’m late! Ugh, this’ll do. Haru-chan’s older, after all.”

“Leaving already? It’s not even 9.”

“Hehe, picking up Haru-chan and dressing her takes time, right?”

“Right…”

With that, Himeko slipped on favorite sandals and dashed out, likely storming Haruki’s house with that energy.

Seeing her off, Hayato sighed in relief, slumping onto the sofa and stretching. The unpacked, tidy living room mirrored his own. Himeko’s room was still half-boxed, but that wasn’t his job.

There were other tasks, though.

“Alright, let’s do this. Trash pickup’s Monday—”

Sorting garbage, cleaning shared spaces like the bathroom and hallway, washing piled-up laundry, including bedsheets—Hayato worked efficiently, his movements practiced. Only hanging Himeko’s underwear creased his brow; even as her brother, handling a girl’s undergarments felt awkward.

Housework wrapped up quickly—the apartment was smaller than their Tsukino-se home. It was just before 11 a.m., too early for lunch, and cooking for one felt pointless. The fridge was predictably empty, forcing a trip out.

“…Might as well.”

After hesitating, Hayato muttered to himself, grabbing an envelope from the living room shelf. He booted up the laptop to check the address written on it, his face solemn.

Leaving the apartment, Hayato headed to the nearest station—an unremarkable building. Marveling at the ten-minute walk and ten hourly trains, he rode two stops, shocked at how short each was. Descending, he aimed for a large white building visible from the platform.

In a suburban area, it featured a vast parking lot and a plaza-like space with lawns and flowerbeds. Despite the greenery, it felt oddly sterile.

Entering the wide lobby, Hayato checked in at reception and headed to the sixth floor, room 617—his mother’s hospital room.

“Oh, Hayato?” his mother, thinner and frail, was peeling a pear with a fruit knife.

“Want some? I got carried away practicing for rehab.”

“Sure… That’s a lot!?”

“Haha, three pears’ worth!”

“Can we finish this? What were you thinking?”

“It’s fine. Dad can eat leftovers tonight.”

“Dad…”

Hayato took a slice, intricately carved into leaf or rabbit shapes, from his cheerful mother. The crisp texture and sweet-tart flavor filled his mouth. She watched him eat fondly, and, embarrassed by her gaze, he turned away, asking what nagged him.

“Uh, you doing… okay?”

“Surgery went well. Some finger numbness, but I’ll move to rehab soon. How’s Himeko? Not crying?”

“She’s fine. Out with a friend today—Haru.”

“Good.”

Her visible relief mirrored Hayato’s at seeing her healthier than expected. Father had said she was fine, but seeing it eased his mind.

“Alright, I’m heading back.”

“Stay longer!”

“Nah.”

Having devoured three pears, Hayato left his reluctant mother, eager to escape.

Her collapse—her second, in front of Himeko—lingered in his mind. Hospitals unnerved him: sterile, detached from normalcy, reeking of disinfectant, decorated with vibrant cut flowers that felt like displayed heads. To Hayato, they were warped spaces.

“Oh.”

“Huh?”

He froze, stunned.

A petite girl with curly, sheep-like hair held it—a quirky classmate who grew vegetables in the school garden.

“Mi-Mitake-san?”

“Kirishima-san…?”

It was a vivid bouquet of white, yellow, and purple vegetable flowers, more a compact buke than a loose arrangement. Zucchini’s male flowers formed the core, surrounded by woven eggplant, tomato, and other tiny blooms.

Even Hayato, clueless about flowers, saw the intricate effort. Above all—

“It’s cute.”

“…What!?”

“So lively with all those flowers. Haha, vegetable flowers look totally different like this.”

“I-I trimmed them and thought this would…!”

“Impressive, Mitake-san.”

“!”

Hayato was genuinely awed. Why vegetable flowers? Why was Mitake here? Questions swirled, but the buke’s vibrancy and Mitake’s creation captivated him, earning his admiring stare.

“Auu…” Mitake faltered.

Blindsided by “cute” and his intense gaze, her mind blanked. Teary, she could only whimper “auu,” like a distressed creature.

(Yep, just like Gen-jii’s sheep in Tsukino-se, bleating when startled.)

Stifling a chuckle, Hayato’s amusement seemed to her like mockery, deepening her blush. Flustered, she fidgeted—then something flew at him.

“Whoa!”

“Eek!”

Catching it, he saw a cola bottle. Scanning around, a red-faced elderly patient in a hospital gown stood in an overhand throw stance, trembling.

“You brat! What’re you doing to my Minamo!?”

“Grandpa!”

“W-What!?”

Seeing Mitake’s buke and the elderly patient, Hayato pieced it together.

“Hey, kid, stay put! Making Minamo cry—what did you do!? Answer, or else!”

“No, I just—!”

“Wait, Grandpa, it’s not like that!”

Misinterpreting, the old man brandished his walking stick. His face, as red as his granddaughter’s, contrasted Hayato’s calming clarity, who thought, Family trait? But the man’s ferocity—sharper than a seasoned hunter’s—made Hayato flinch. The stick’s swings were fierce for an elderly patient.

“You dare mess with my granddaughter!?”

“Ow, ouch!”

“Grandpa!”

Clearing the misunderstanding took considerable effort.

Minutes later, in a nearby ward.

Mitake’s grandfather bowed to Hayato.

“Sorry, kid. We good?”

“Uh, sure…”

“Mitake, that half-hearted apology’ll get you in trouble with Minamo-chan.”

“Yeah, old man, she might stop talking to you!”

“Grr… Sorry, my bad! Forgive me, kid!”

“Uh, sure…”

His reluctance was clear, egged on by wardmates, his dissatisfaction obvious.

“Easy, Mitake. Hey, kid, you the teacher showing Minamo-chan about vegetables?”

“Teacher?”

“Thanks to you, she’s been smiling more. Appreciate it.”

“Even this old guy’s secretly grateful.”

“…Hmph.”

“Uh, sure…”

The grandfather’s sharp, hostile glare marked him as tough, but the teasing suggested he was softer without Minamo involved. She was clearly adored here, likely a frequent visitor.

“So, kid, a transfer student, huh? Why’d you talk to her?”

“Huh?”

“Why approach our Minamo?”

“Well…”

“She’s cute, I get it. A wingless angel. Wanting to chat’s understandable. Flirting? A fling? If so—”

“No, that’s not it!”

His intensity was overwhelming. A cane thrust at Hayato’s face drew a strangled yelp. Feeling his life on the line, he blurted the truth.

“She reminded me of a kid from my old town! I looked after her a lot, and her panicking’s just like her, so I couldn’t ignore her!”

“Oh? Minamo did? What kid?”

“An 8-year-old girl with curly hair!”

“And you spoke to her because of that?”

“Y-Yes!”

The grandfather looked unconvinced, but wardmates murmured agreement: “Makes sense,” “She’s clumsy,” “Makes you wanna give her candy.”

Hayato laughed awkwardly. The “8-year-old” was a sheep, long past its prime, but he didn’t clarify.

Minamo returned from changing vase water.

“Grandpa, where’s the bouquet… Grandpa! What’re you doing to Kirishima-san!”

“Minamo, it’s just—”

“Grandpa! Stop bothering him! Let’s go!”

“Minamo!”

“Pfft, old man’s jealous at his age.”

“See ya, Minamo-chan!”

Shocked at her grandfather threatening Hayato, Minamo grabbed his hand, dragging him out with uncharacteristic strength, her ears and neck flushed with embarrassment.

Hayato, understanding her mortification at family antics, followed. But in the lobby, being led by a girl felt awkward.

“Mitake-san, uh, your hand…”

“Oh! S-Sorry!”

“No, it’s fine.”

It was his first time holding a girl’s hand, aside from Himeko or Haruki. Realizing this, his face heated.

To deflect, he said, “Quite a lively grandpa, huh?”

“…Yeah.”

It was a casual remark, based on the energetic man he saw. But this was a hospital, and her grandfather was a patient.

Too late, Hayato realized his mistake.

Minamo’s embarrassed flush darkened, her face on the verge of tears. In a flash, she forced her usual soft smile, unsettling Hayato further. He cursed his tactlessness.

“…Sorry.”

“No, it’s not your fault… My grandpa was…”

“Uh, yeah, haha.”

“Mm.”

They apologized, sharing wry smiles. Her fleeting dark expression gave way to a resolute strength, making that moment stand out more.

(That face…)

It reminded him of the day his mother collapsed in childhood, when Himeko stood frozen, lost, unable to act.

He couldn’t ignore Minamo.

“Well, Kirishima-san, I’ll—”

“Wait, Mitake-san!”

“Yes?”

“Uh… The garden! Can I visit the garden again?”

“Fwe?”

The unexpected request made her blink. Comprehension dawned, and her face lit up.

“Yes!”

“Cool, thanks.”

Her bright smile met his vague one. He knew his offer was somewhat selfish.

With that, he hurried out. A strong wind blasted, washing away the disinfectant smell.

Cumulonimbus clouds rose in the early summer sky.
