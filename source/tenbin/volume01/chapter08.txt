Episode 8: Getting Cheeky
In Himeko’s room, filled with pastel furniture, cute trinkets, and scattered cardboard boxes, the Kirishima siblings’ morning battle unfolded.

“Get up, Himeko! Come on, it’s past 8!”

“Mmm… cold shabu salad udon…”

“Fine, I’ll make it tonight, just get up!!”

“Sesame dressing…”

“Himeko!”

Himeko occasionally overslept massively. Hayato always woke her, but today she was especially stubborn. Shouting and shaking did nothing; only slamming her, blanket and all, onto the floor elicited a “Fgyah!” as she finally woke.

They scrambled to get ready and bolted out the door.

“Ugh, my hair’s a mess! I’m starving! Sweaty from the morning—worst day ever!”

“I told you to sleep early last night!”

“But—!”

Himeko’s late-night habits were to blame. Hayato had urged her to sleep, but her phone’s notification pings echoed from her room past midnight. Entirely her fault.

(Tch, I’ll sneak raw tomatoes into dinner for revenge!)

Hayato vowed to retaliate with her least favorite food.

Hayato burst into the classroom just before the bell.

“Yo, Kirishima. Overslept?”

“Not me, my sister, Mori.”

“Oh, you’ve got a sister? How old?”

“One year younger, third-year middle school… What’s that?”

“Hard to explain.”

They stared at a scene where classmates swarmed one girl like pilgrims. It was Haruki, her face tinged with melancholy. Concerned peers took turns checking on her.

“You okay, Nikaidou-san?”

“Tell us if something’s wrong.”

“I’m fine. Just couldn’t sleep last night…”

Her quiet smile, marred by puffy eyes, suggested she was bravely hiding pain, fueling everyone’s worry.

(That jerk!)

To Hayato, it screamed late-night chats with Himeko. He rubbed his brow in frustration.

“…Oh.”

Haruki’s phone pinged. She fidgeted, read the message, and giggled softly—a move potent enough to stir the room.

“Couldn’t sleep last night… that happy face now… no way…!”

“Wait, paradoxically, isn’t it weirder if she didn’t have someone?”

“Where’s the transfer student? Find Kirishima! If he’s got a phone, he’s guilty!”

Some boys, practically murderous, began forming a posse to confront Hayato.

“…Ah.”

Haruki finally realized her words and actions were sparking misunderstandings. Her arched brows furrowed as her eyes met Hayato’s.

“(What do I do!?)”

“(…Tch, that’s a debt.)”

Hayato sighed dramatically, nodding at her subtle plea, and headed to his seat.

“Morning, Nikaidou. You look thrilled with your phone. Got a girlfriend?”

“G-Girlfriend!? No, I— Hayato…?”

“Nah, you just seem like you’re giddy over a new girlfriend or maybe reconnecting with someone special.”

“Uh…”

Hayato’s sudden jab threw her off, and the class, intrigued by the juicy topic, watched intently. Winking meaningfully, Hayato signaled his intent. Haruki, catching on, smirked and showed her phone.

“Exactly! Look—cute, right? We reunited after years, and last night we got lost in memories…”

“Oh, uh, yeah, cute… girl, huh…?”

The screen showed a selfie from last night at Hayato’s place. Himeko, dressed and made-up for the convenience store, looked stunning, even without sibling bias.

Peers peeking over remarked, “Oh, cute!” “You’re close, huh?” “What school’s she at?”—positive reactions suggesting Himeko’s looks held up. The tense air around Haruki dissipated, and with the teacher’s “Quiet down, take your seats!” it vanished completely.

During roll call, Haruki mouthed to Hayato.

“(Thanks.)”

Her smile revealed faint dark circles—proof of last night’s late chat. Hayato wanted to snap, “Idiot,” but the memory of her dark, empty house silenced him. His chest tightened inexplicably.

“(…Keep it in check.)”

He muttered softly. Haruki smiled back, sheepish and embarrassed.

“Man, you saved me, Hayato!”

Lunchtime, at their secret base. Haruki slapped Hayato’s back, perhaps to hide her morning embarrassment.

“Ow, ow! Ease up… What’s that?”

“Nude cushion?”

She pulled out two small, coverless white cushions from her bag, slightly squished but slowly puffing up.

“Tiny ones without covers. Got ‘em at the dollar store. 200 yen, though. Here’s yours.”

“200 yen at a dollar store!? Oh, money—”

“It’s fine, call it a debt. But… I’ve got a little favor.”

“Hm? What—hey!”

Hugging the cushion to her chest, Haruki blushed, fidgeting and sidling up with an upward gaze—girlishly cute but with mischievous eyes, clearly joking.

Even knowing it was a prank, her charm made Hayato’s heart skip. He leaned back theatrically to hide it, but her grin widened, and she inched closer, pressing her advantage.

“I want something of yours, Hayato…”

“W-What!?”

“You really want me to say it…?”

“Haruki, you…”

Her suggestive words, even in jest, flustered him beyond hiding. Knowing this, she touched her lips sensually, then traced his hand holding his bag. A shiver ran down his spine. Her playful, almost bewitching eyes overwhelmed him. Swallowing hard, he gave in.

“Hayato…”

“Haruki…”

Grrroooowl.

A loud stomach rumble shattered the moment.

The earlier tension gone, Hayato looked at Haruki, who hid her face behind the cushion, embarrassed.

“I-I was rushed this morning! No breakfast, couldn’t buy lunch!”

“Sigh… Another debt. I’ll share, but no chopsticks. Can’t lend mine…”

“Oh, I’ve got spares from the convenience store in my bag.”

“…Right.”

Hayato, torn between disappointment and relief, divided his bento. Splitting it was tough for a growing teen, but unavoidable.

Something else nagged him. Haruki’s bulk frozen food buys at the supermarket, her convenience store dinner run, the dark house last night—unpleasant possibilities loomed.

“Mmm, this veggie-packed hamburger’s delicious. You made it?”

“Yeah, froze a batch the other day.”

“Frozen’s different? You looked weird.”

“Huh?”

Her comment made Hayato realize his thoughts had shown. Before him was the childhood friend he’d shared popsicles with, laughing together.

They were sharing lunch like old times, but his grim expression worried Haruki. Her timid, almost scared look—like a child fearing a scolding—shook him more than her earlier teasing.

“Sorry, I—”

“—I was just stunned. Staying up late chatting—what were you even talking about?”

“Hayato… Haha, lots. Hime-chan’s super into fashion and makeup. I knew nothing, so she scolded me.”

“Really? With your perfect act, I figured…”

“It’s just playing the good girl. And… well…”

“…Haruki?”

“N-Nothing!”

She forced a dodge, her attitude screaming not to pry. Her fleeting dark expression mirrored last night’s farewell.

(That doesn’t suit you!)

To Hayato, Haruki was always mischievous, pushy, endlessly bright, and smiling—unchanged after seven years. But those seven years built walls of unknowns, separating “Haruki” then and now.

He didn’t know her struggles, likely too heavy to share casually. Understanding this, he hesitated to push. Yet, he couldn’t ignore his concern, and Himeko’s past pain flashed in his mind. Before he knew it, he was roughly ruffling Haruki’s hair.

“Wah! Hayato, what’s that for!?”

Startled, she protested. But seeing his face, she fell silent, letting him muss her carefully styled hair.

Hayato’s expression was complex. Her once short, sun-damaged hair was now long, silky, tickling his fingers.

“Haruki—”

You okay?

Need to talk, I’m here.

I’m right here…

Words rose and faded, none feeling right. His emotions spun, frustratingly inarticulate.

Haruki, watching his shifting expressions, giggled and said simply:

“Yeah.”

A brief exchange, but enough for them. For now, this sufficed. Her face looked content.

“You’ve changed, Hayato.”

“Have I?”

“Yup. You’ve gotten cheeky.”

“Huh? What’s that mean?”

“Haha, who knows?”

Outside, early summer clouds curled, just like back then.

Rural and urban, years apart, frustrations with changes. Yet, laughing, they shared the same meal, just like before.
