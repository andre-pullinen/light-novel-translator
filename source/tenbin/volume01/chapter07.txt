Episode 7: Only Child
It was during dinner that evening.

“Onii, I wanna go buy ice cream.”

“Hm? We’ve got some in stock, don’t we?”

Himeko’s sudden declaration caught Hayato off guard. Not grasping her meaning, he twirled pasta on his fork, pleased with its taste.

Tonight’s dinner was pasta with a ratatouille sauce starring gifted tomatoes, eggplants, and zucchinis. Garlic-infused olive oil, vibrant with paprika and celery, made it a colorful dish.

“Onii, I don’t just want ice cream—I want to go buy it.”

“Sorry, I’m not following. Just go, then?”

“At night. To a convenience store.”

“!”

“I wanna shop at a convenience store at night.”

“What…?”

Nighttime convenience stores held special meaning for Hayato and Himeko, who’d only known a rural “convenience store” in Tsukino-se that opened at 11 a.m. and closed by 7 p.m. With no shops open at night there, the idea of casually shopping after dark was thrilling, stirring excitement in both siblings.

“You’ll come with me, right, Onii?”

“A nighttime convenience store, huh? Gotta experience it once.”

“Ugh, I’m nervous. What should I wear? Uniform’s safe, but it’s late—will we get stopped?”

“Can’t you check online?”

“Maybe?”

The two buzzed with unusual enthusiasm.

It was just before 8 p.m.

In Tsukino-se, it’d be pitch-black, but in the city, it was still early evening. For Hayato and Himeko, leaving the house at this hour was rare, reserved for emergencies.

A mix of guilt, like doing something forbidden, and the thrill of an adventure made their hearts race.

“No need for a flashlight here.”

“It’s bright, but no stars.”

“Still, Himeko, that outfit…”

Her summery off-shoulder top, flared miniskirt, and light makeup screamed date-night, not a convenience store run—unlike Hayato’s plain shirt and jeans.

“Hmph, I went all out. Don’t wanna look like a country bumpkin!”

“Sigh…”

They walked through the night-lit residential area. Streetlights replaced stars, car engines drowned out bugs and frogs. The city’s nighttime face felt like another world.

Himeko, nervous, clutched Hayato’s shirt, following cautiously.

After ten minutes—long enough to heighten their excitement—they reached their destination on the main road.

“Here we are. The convenience store.”

“We made it. It’s really open.”

“Really open.”

At this early hour, it bustled with office workers, students, and loiterers flipping through magazines—an ordinary scene. But to Hayato and Himeko, it was awe-inspiring. Feeling out of place, they hesitated at the entrance.

Exchanging glances—“You going?” “You first!”—Himeko suddenly gasped.

“Whoa, amazing!”

“Hm?”

Following her gaze, Hayato spotted a girl in a loose shirt, skinny jeans, and a cap—a casual “just popped to the store” look. Yet her striking beauty and figure proved a pretty girl could pull off anything. It was Haruki.

“Oh.”

“…Yo.”

Noticing Himeko’s voice, Haruki’s eyes widened, then she approached with a warm smile.

“Good evening, Kirishima-kun. What a coincidence!”

“Yeah, fancy meeting you. Shopping?”

“Yup, just grabbing something… Who’s this adorable girl? Your girlfriend?”

“Huh?”

“Eek!”

Haruki’s charming smile, even disarming to girls, flustered Himeko, who hid behind Hayato, blushing. Haruki’s “How cute!” only deepened her flush, her grip tightening on his shirt.

“Geez, Kirishima-kun, you work fast. Snagging such a cute girlfriend right after transferring.”

“Wait, you’ve got it wrong.”

“Hehe, no need to be shy.”

“U-Um, do you two know each other?”

“Yes, same class, next-door seats. I might know him a bit better than most. I can spill some secrets if you want.”

“I-I’m, um…”

Something was off.

Hayato sighed at their bizarre exchange.

“Of course you know her. Himeko, this is Haruki. Haruki, Himeko.”

“What?”

“Huh?”

Time froze. Haruki and Himeko stared at each other.

“Haru… chan?”

“Hime… chan?”

““EHHHH!?””

Their shouts echoed at the convenience store entrance.

Twenty minutes later, Haruki was at the Kirishima dining table, devouring ratatouille pasta.

“So good! It’s infuriatingly good, and it’s Hayato!”

“Right? Onii’s cooking is too good, so my skills are stuck at zero.”

“I get it!”

“Don’t just get it, Haruki. And learn, Himeko.”

Haruki had come to buy a bento, so Himeko invited her over—partly because their loud scene at the store got awkward.

Watching Haruki eat eagerly despite her grumbling, Hayato’s expression softened.

“Delicious, thanks! I had seconds! If I gain weight, it’s your fault, Hayato!”

“Onii’s food is tasty but always rice-friendly… Total girl enemy.”

“Yup, enemy of women.”

“Right?”

“Right.”

“Ugh…”

Haruki and Himeko nodded in sync. Their initial tension and formality vanished, chatting like lifelong friends despite seven years apart.

In Tsukino-se, they were among the few kids their age, often trailing Hayato in play. That bond, though stretched by time and distance, still lived, their faces naturally mirroring childhood smiles.

“Haru-chan, you’re a total beauty now! I didn’t recognize you at first, I was shocked!”

“Haha, my disguise is pretty good, huh?”

“Totally fooled! I thought you were a boy back then. Onii said you hadn’t changed, just powered up from monkey to gorilla.”

“Grr, Hayato!?”

“Oops, dishes to wash.”

“That’s a debt!”

Haruki’s glare startled Hayato, who fled to the kitchen. Like Himeko, he’d assumed Haruki was a boy, and the embarrassment made him scrub plates to dodge it. Haruki and Himeko exchanged amused glances.

“…Yeah.”

“What’s up, Hime-chan?”

“Just… like Onii said, you’re still you. Hard to explain, but yeah.”

“Haha, what? But… Hayato said that too, huh?”

“Oh, Haru-chan, let’s exchange contacts! Unlike Onii, I’ve got a phone. Unlike Onii.”

“Yup, let’s! It’s Hayato who’s weird for not having one.”

“Right? Onii’s always been like—”

“Yeah, Hayato’s just—”

Haruki and Himeko, reunited childhood friends, bonded over Hayato. Rural and urban, seven years of gaps— their talk was endless.

Laughter filled the night, time flying.

“Ugh, I should head back… Oh, where’re your parents?”

“Late again, probably.”

“Got it. Thanks for having me.”

“Text me, Haru-chan!”

Past 9 p.m., Haruki stood, feeling she’d overstayed. Himeko, and Hayato too, felt reluctant to part.

“I’ll walk you.”

“Hayato?”

His sudden offer surprised them both. Neither knew why he said it, their dumbfounded faces comical, but Himeko nodded approvingly.

“Yeah, Onii, walk her home. Haru-chan’s so cute, you gotta.”

“W-What!? N-No, I’m fine! It’s a quick run, I don’t need—”

“Uh, well… It’s a chance to rack up a debt, you know.”

“Hayato!”

Mumbling an excuse, Hayato stepped out, Haruki hurrying after, leaving the Kirishima house.

“Ugh, forcing a debt on me, jeez!”

Despite her grumbling, her face seemed pleased.

Unlike the busy convenience store route, Haruki’s residential street was quiet, with few people or cars. Lights spilled from homes, likely filled with family time.

Hayato and Haruki walked side-by-side, an odd but pleasant feeling settling in.

“By the way.”

“Hm?”

“Hime-chan’s gotten cute.”

“Has she? I wouldn’t know.”

“Lucky you. I always wanted a sibling like that.”

“It’s not all great. She oversleeps, acts big, is selfish, hogs the TV…”

“Haha, I can totally picture that. So Hime-chan.”

“Right?”

“But—oh, we’re here.”

“Yup.”

Haruki’s house, an ordinary home, looked distorted to Hayato, its windows dark.

“You’ve got Himeko, she’s got you. I’m a bit jealous.”

“Haruki…?”

Saying this casually, she pulled out her key, stepping into the darkness.

“I’m an only child, you know.”

With a slightly lonely smile, she turned back.

Hayato wanted to say something but found no words.

“Goodnight, Hayato. See ya!”

“Y-Yeah, see ya, Haruki.”

Emphasizing see ya, she closed the door.

An unsettled feeling lingered. Hayato scratched his head, hand raised in farewell, unsure why it felt so heavy.
