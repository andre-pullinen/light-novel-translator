Episode 6: I Told You So, Didn’t I?
It was raining since morning.

The steady drizzle from last night cast a gloomy mood. Few would want to go to work or school on such a day.

“Ugh! My hair’s a mess!”

At the Kirishima household, Himeko’s protests rang out against the dreary vibe.

Hayato, treating her complaints as background noise, thought, Same old, same old, while preparing bentos: reheated frozen karaage, leftover kinpira of burdock and carrots, and mini tomatoes.

“Rainy days suck! My hair frizzes, and the top flattens!”

“I kinda like them. No drought worries or farm duties.”

“Onii, there’re no fields here…”

“Oh, right.”

“Anyway, the app says it’ll clear by noon. Should I bring a folding umbrella to avoid forgetting it?”

“Smartphone?”

“Yup. Pretty accurate and handy.”

“Sounds useful.”

“You, interested? That’s rare.”

“…Haruki chewed me out for not having one.”

“Oh, Haru-chan…”

Himeko nodded knowingly, arms crossed, her gaze mixing disapproval and exasperation.

“Listen, Onii. Smartphones are essential now. Like fridges in homes or tractors on farms—everyone’s supposed to have one. You’re the weird one.”

“Seriously? That big a deal?”

“Tsukino-se barely got one bar, but it’s still useful. I chat with Saki-chan daily.”

“Oh, Murao-san.”

Murao Saki, a rare peer in Tsukino-se, Himeko’s classmate and friend. Quiet and shy, she’d hide behind Himeko when Hayato approached, a reserved friend Himeko cherished.

(Got it…)

The move separated Himeko and Saki. Tsukino-se was hours away by car—not a casual trip. Without licenses or cars, it was a full-day journey for them, almost an eternal parting for kids.

Yet Himeko stayed in touch. A palm-sized device offered a fragile but real connection.

“So… Murao-san’s doing okay?”

“Same as ever? Oh, she seemed worried about you. Did something happen?”

“Me? If anything, she avoided me.”

“Right? She always acted weird around you. Instinct, maybe?”

“Stop, Himeko. That hurts.”

“Haha, I’ll hype you up to her then.”

“Please.”

Himeko typed a message. Watching, Hayato wondered if staying connected with Haruki via phone back then would’ve changed their future.

As she typed, Himeko spoke.

“Haru-chan was probably thrilled to see you after so long. You’re still friends, right? She likely wants to catch up more.”

“Maybe… Yeah.”

Honestly, Hayato didn’t know why Haruki yelled yesterday. He realized he knew little about her—her being a girl, her diorama hobby, potential misunderstandings. Still, she was a special childhood friend.

(Would a phone help us understand each other better?)

Hayato began seriously considering getting one.

Early summer rain pattered on the asphalt commute.

Colorful umbrellas dotted the scene, many flashy. Himeko, with her plain black folding umbrella, grumbled, “Ugh, my girl power…!” as she headed to middle school.

Seeing her off, Hayato admired the paved roads—free of rural mud or puddles—and passed the school gate, spotting the garden.

Days after pollinating the zucchinis, they should be fruiting. Curiosity drew him to check.

There, the curly-haired girl tended vegetables, unbothered by the rain.

“Looking good. Some are ready to harvest.”

“…Oh!”

“How’s it going?”

“Look! The zucchinis grew well, and the pruned tomatoes and eggplants… Um…”

“Can I borrow the pruning shears? Let’s pick these… Got a bag?”

“Yes, a plastic one!”

Besides zucchinis, other fruits had formed, some overripe—likely missed due to inexperience. Hayato explained harvest timing while snipping, a familiar task for him but new to her. Her face was earnest.

The garden, though large for a flowerbed, was small for a plot, and harvesting took minutes. The bag brimmed with produce.

The girl gazed at it with deep emotion—her first harvest, a profound milestone.

“Um, take half! Please?”

“Really? I’d appreciate it.”

“It’s thanks to you they’re harvested.”

“No big deal… But thanks.”

“Yes!”

Her radiant smile was infectious, warming Hayato.

After dividing the veggies into another bag, she took a deep breath, looking up nervously.

“U-Um!”

“W-What!?”

Hayato had few female peers. Being looked up at by a shy, earnest girl, especially with their height difference, was flustering. Her serious gaze held him.

“C-Could you share your contact or ID!? I-I don’t have anyone else to ask, and I have questions… I know it’s sudden, and I don’t want to bother you, but if it’s okay…”

“…”

Her rapid words, flushed face, and tightly gripped phone showed her resolve. It pained him to see her desperation, a monumental effort for her.

But he had only one response.

“Uh… Sorry.”

“I… see…”

“No, wait! It’s not like that!”

“Huh?”

“I don’t have a phone… I’m a transfer from a no-signal countryside, so…”

Now Hayato scrambled to explain, gesturing wildly to convey he simply didn’t own one.

His sincerity reached her. Initially stunned, she soon giggled. Hayato scratched his head awkwardly.

“I’m Mitake from Class C. Minamo Mitake.”

“Kirishima Hayato, Class A. I’ll let you know when I get a phone.”

“Promise?”

“—! Y-Yeah… Promise.”

The word promise startled him, carrying special weight. He hesitated, Haruki’s face flashing in his mind.

But objecting felt unnatural. It was just about exchanging contacts later. No big deal—he told himself, accepting the veggies.

“See you.”

“Yeah…”

Late June, under summer rain, with vegetable flowers as witnesses, an unexpected vow was made.

Hayato reached the classroom five minutes before homeroom, soaked from harvesting without an umbrella.

“Water-dripping heartthrob, eh, Kirishima?”

“Shut it, Mori.”

“What’s with the haul?”

“Veggies. A gift.”

“How do you even get that…?”

“Don’t you?”

“No way!”

Chatting, Hayato sat. The light rain had soaked his uniform, sticking to his shoulders, with droplets falling from his hair. Uncomfortable, but it’d dry.

“You okay, Kirishima-kun?”

“Oh, yeah…”

Haruki’s concerned voice came. Known school-wide for kindness, her speaking to a seatmate was natural.

But her slight smirk, like a kid with a toy, didn’t escape Hayato. Bracing for mischief, he tensed.

“No umbrella? Use this.”

“N-No, I’m good! It’ll dry soon.”

“You’ll catch a cold.”

“Wait, Haru—Nikaidou!!”

Shockingly, Haruki began wiping his face with her handkerchief. An idol-like beauty showing concern and personally drying him sparked chaos—chairs clattered, some boys even dashed into the rain.

(This jerk!)

Her smug grin confirmed she was trolling. The classroom’s mix of jealousy, shock, and agitation pierced Hayato.

Unable to bear it, he snatched the handkerchief.

“T-Thanks! I’ll wash and return it!”

“You can just give it back.”

“N-No way! I’ll wash it, okay!?”

“Fine… Then, for smooth return, share your phone contact…”

“What, you—!”

Her pushy move ignored obvious retorts—like returning it tomorrow or knowing he had no phone. Somehow, it became Nikaidou Haruki leveraging this to get the transfer student’s contact.

Stinging gazes bore into Hayato. Haruki’s downcast act, paired with a smirk only he saw, taunted him.

“S-Sorry, I don’t have a phone… Hey!”

“Oh… Sorry, I shouldn’t have asked…”

“No, wait! I really don’t have one!”

His excuses were for the class, who whispered, “He’s that desperate to avoid her?” “Poor Nikaidou-san…”

No matter how he insisted, they didn’t believe him, fueling their—especially the boys’—ire.

“Kirishima, let’s talk.”

“You’re new, so we don’t know each other well yet.”

“Just answer some simple questions.”

“Wait, I— Mori, you traitor!”

“Haha!”

Dragged off for a “talk,” Hayato caught Haruki’s satisfied look, her tongue playfully out.

After school, Haruki dragged him to her place.

“See? I told you, you’re the weird one for not having a phone—there!”

“Got it. I had a rough time. And your popularity—yoink!”

“Hehe, I work hard for it… Ugh, my HP!”

“Overcommitted. Healing… Oh, no MP.”

“Nooo!”

They were playing a new action RPG, her idea. Playing together seemed odd, but Haruki planned to progress only with Hayato.

A remake of a game from before their birth, Haruki controlled a beastman fighter, Hayato a mercenary swordsman. They relied on CPU healing, playing recklessly.

Their aggressive style led to a wipeout, and they laughed at their recklessness.

“By the way, those veggies?”

“From Mitake. A thank-you… Want some?”

“…Nah, I’d feel bad if I couldn’t finish them alone.”

“Hm? Okay.”

Her tone felt off, but she quickly shifted back.

“So, why no phone? Family rule?”

“Nah, Himeko’s a pro with hers… Oh, she wants to see you. Come over sometime.”

“Hime-chan!? Totally! …But seriously, why no phone?”

“Ugh… Well…”

“Staaare…”

“Uh, just… didn’t get around to it.”

“Huh!?”

Haruki’s incredulous shout filled her room. Hayato looked away, sheepish.

“It’s so convenient! Didn’t people use them back there?”

“Y-Yeah… Apps for farm management, tractor videos online…”

“Can you not laugh, Haruki-san?”

“Fine, what is it, Hayato-kun?”

“There’re too many types… I got overwhelmed and…”

“Pfft… Haha!”

“Don’t laugh! I’m serious! That’s five debts!”

“Sorry, sorry. So, too many choices, and you froze?”

“Got a problem with that?”

Timing was part of it, sure.

“Hehe, alright. Let’s pick one out this weekend.”

“Really?”

“Yup, it’s a promise.”

“…Yeah!”

They laughed, faces meeting. Planning the weekend over games felt just like old times.
