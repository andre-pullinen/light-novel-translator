Episode 10: Himeko’s Resolve
Twenty minutes’ walk from the apartment.

Unlike Tsukino-se’s rural combined elementary-middle school or its wooden single-story building, Himeko’s middle school was a three-story concrete structure. Her favorite uniform—a checkered skirt and sailor top with matching collar—replaced the old, frumpy jumper skirt.

Himeko stood out as a striking beauty even in this urban school. Her spirited, bright eyes, wavy hair, and slender figure (with some parts still developing) drew attention, amplified by her transfer-student status.

Rushing into class just before being late, she sighed wistfully, catching eyes.

Her new friend, Honoka Torikai, immediately pounced.

“Himeko-chan, that sigh… Something up?”

“Uh, yeah, kinda.”

“TV schedule got messed up again?”

“Missed a week and freaked, but I’m good now.”

“Missing wooden telephone poles?”

“Getting used to that.”

“No dead frogs or lizards on the roads?”

“They really don’t show up here—wait, I’m not a country bumpkin!”

“Haha, right!”

Blushing, Himeko stood to protest, but Honoka and classmates watched warmly. Though Himeko hid her rural roots, her shock at no coin-operated rice polishers and ignorance of private railways exposed her on day one, cementing her as the class’s lovable mascot.

She still thought her origins were a secret.

“So, what’s up? Another shock from your old town?”

“Kinda. Not a thing—a person.”

“Person?”

“My brother’s old friend. I saw them again after years, and they’ve changed so much, it’s… messing with my head.”

“What were they like?”

“Hm… They ran wild with Onii, loved pranks, got scolded a lot, but still looked out for me. A total rascal. Now they’re, like, a perfect honor student type.”

“Hmm… Did you like them?”

“Uh… Yeah, maybe. I think I did.”

Like.

The word settled heavily in Himeko’s chest. A vague, childish affection—too hazy to call first love, yet fitting the term like. She’d definitely liked Haruki, thinking they were a boy.

But even if those feelings were real, they could never be. Haruki was a girl, an unchangeable fact. A bittersweet ache stabbed her chest, and she clutched it unconsciously.

“That’s unfair…”

“Himeko-chan…?”

Seeing her childhood friend transformed, no trace of the rough-and-tumble leader, now stunningly refined, yet standing by her brother’s side—it felt unfair. No other word fit.

Honoka, seeing Himeko’s pained murmur, couldn’t tease. Instead, her romantic instincts flared, urging her to meddle. Other girls, sensing the same, swarmed Himeko.

“Kirishima-san, tell us more about them!”

“Any stories that show who they are?”

“What’s ‘unfair’ mean? How do you feel now?”

“You gotta tell them your feelings, or you’ll regret it!”

“Uh, um… Regret…?”

In Tsukino-se, Himeko never faced a gaggle of same-age girls. Overwhelmed, she spun under their barrage.

One word stuck: regret.

Himeko had a deep regret—Saki.

Her only true peer in Tsukino-se, a lifelong friend. The sudden move meant Himeko couldn’t tell her until the last moment.

“I wish you’d told me sooner! I wanted to do so much more with you, Hime-chan, and with your brother…”

Saki’s sobs were uncontrollable. Calming her was tough; they ended up crying together. Though they still talked, forgiving each other, the physical distance was insurmountable.

Saki and Hayato’s awkward tension lingered, unresolved by the move. Thinking of Saki intensified Himeko’s regret.

(Saki-chan still cares about Onii…)

That thought sparked urgency. She couldn’t sit still. Slapping her cheeks, she stood.

“Alright, I’m doing this! I’ll talk to Haru-chan, get close, become best friends!”

Raising her fist with an “Ei-ei-oh!” Honoka and the girls cheered, clapping wildly, their eyes gleaming like predators spotting prey.

“Yes, let’s plan, Himeko-kun.”

“Tell us more, we’ll help!”

“Time for a tapioca meeting!”

“Tapioca? Nobody sells that anymore!”

“Uh, wait… Tapioca!? The trendy drink!? No way, I wanna try it!”

“““…”””

Tapioca was long past its prime. The girls meant it as a joke, but Himeko latched on eagerly, embodying rural trends’ delayed arrival.

“That’s our Kirishima-san!”

“I’m treating!”

“Stay like this forever, Himeko-chan!”

“Huh? Guys?”

Confused by their reactions, Himeko faced warm, amused smiles, some stifling laughter. Thus, she solidified her mascot status further.

◇◇◇

Friday, before the weekend when Hayato would pick a smartphone with Haruki, the last weekday’s muggy evening persisted from a steamy morning.

“Ugh, I’m back… Onii, ice cream!”

“In the freezer, grab it.”

“Hey, Hime-chan, welcome back!”

Sweaty Himeko returned to the air-conditioned apartment, greeted by Hayato cooking dinner and Haruki sprawled on the living room sofa, reading manga.

Haruki, keeping her promise to join them for dinner, was already at their place. Familiarity bred no restraint—perhaps loneliness played a part too.

Her state was appalling, making Himeko frown. Hugging a cushion, lying face-down, skirt flipped up carelessly, socks off, feet kicking—the lax posture revealed plain, navy boxer-style shorts, utterly unsexy.

“Haru-chan, that…”

“Hm? Oh, this! It’s that Taisho-era manga that got an anime. Was sold out forever, but I finally got it. Come read with me!”

“No, I mean…”

“Huh…?”

Haruki’s clueless tilt was maddeningly cute yet pitiful. Himeko palmed her forehead.

Glancing at Hayato, he chopped vegetables, unfazed, as if this was normal. Their unchanged dynamic, for better or worse, made Himeko’s lips curl despite her exasperation.

“Hime-chan, come on!”

“Ugh!”

Sitting up, Haruki patted the sofa, eyes sparkling, manga in hand. Reluctantly, Himeko joined her.

The shonen battle manga gripped both girls, pulling them in completely.

“Haru-chan, where’s volume two!?”

“Here! Welcome to the abyss!”

“What are they doing…?” Hayato muttered.

They read silently until dinner was ready.

“Itadakimasu!”

“Onii, this…”

“Eat the tomatoes, Himeko.”

Dinner was cold shabu salad udon: chilled thin noodles with mizuna, baby leaves, leeks, cucumber, daikon sprouts, tomatoes, and cold pork slices, served with ponzu, sesame dressing, or other options.

The heat dulled appetites, but the light dish went down easily. Himeko, grimacing at raw tomatoes, swallowed them under Hayato’s pressure.

Sipping tea post-meal, Himeko suddenly spoke up.

“Oh, Onii, can I borrow Haru-chan tomorrow?”

“Me?”

“Phone shopping’s Sunday, right?”

Himeko knew about Hayato’s smartphone plans and Haruki’s disastrous fashion sense—too egregious for a teenage girl to ignore.

“Yup, let’s have a date, Haru-chan. A friend date.”

“D-D-D-D-Date!? With me!? I’ve never been on a date!”

Haruki flushed, flustered by the word, her inexperience clear. Himeko, resisting the urge to tease, pressed on.

“Not a big deal. You’ve had tons of ‘dates’ with Onii—field exploration dates, river-dive-soaking dates, six-hour cicada-hunting dates, right?”

“Oh, true.”

“Zero romance in those.”

Haruki clapped in realization, while Hayato stifled laughter, recalling those times.

“So, just you and me hanging out?”

“Yup.”

“Cool, what’ll we do? Hayato’s okay being left out? I’d rather we three—”

“Clothes.”

“Three of—”

“We’re buying you clothes, Haru-chan.”

“…Himeko-sama…?”

Himeko’s eyes were deadly serious, brimming with purpose, her smile that of a hunter locking onto prey. Haruki froze, a chill running down her spine.

“Hayato!”

“Time to wash dishes.”

“Traitor!”

Desperate for rescue, Haruki watched Hayato flee to the kitchen, his back screaming, A guy would just get in the way.

Haruki was bad at girly things, avoiding them deliberately.

“I wanna pick clothes with you, Haru-chan, talk more, get closer than ever.”

Himeko’s words were heartfelt. Her earnest gaze softened Haruki’s resistance.

Sighing heavily, she conceded.

“Fine. Let’s date tomorrow.”

“Haru-chan!”

Overjoyed, Himeko grabbed her hand tightly. Feeling cherished by her younger friend warmed Haruki’s heart.

Himeko yanked her up, beaming radiantly.

“Uh, Hime-chan?”

“I’ll lend you my clothes. Let’s pick tomorrow’s outfit!”

“What? Huh? Wait!”

No way you’re going out in those awful clothes, right? Himeko’s forceful aura dragged Haruki to her room for a mandatory fashion show.

“Hm, not quite. Next, this one.”

“Argh, more!?”

That night, Haruki’s plaintive wails echoed through the Kirishima house until late.
