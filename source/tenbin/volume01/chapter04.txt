Episode 4: Things I’m Not Used To, Things I’m Bad At
As a new transfer student, Hayato Kirishima was still adjusting to many things.

Unlike rural Tsukino-se, where he’d pass tractors on the way to school or find deer and boars in the schoolyard, the city was different. Classrooms were packed full.

This extended to gym class too.

“The ball’s over there!”

“No way, you caught that from there?!”

“But you fall for every feint, it’s hilarious!”

“…Tch!”

Today, their class joined another for mixed-gender sports, split into groups for various games. Hayato’s group played soccer.

His farm-honed athleticism stood out, astonishing peers. Yet, his susceptibility to every feint and trick doubled their surprise, earning laughs and warm acceptance.

“Good work, Kirishima. Gotta say, you’re… something, haha!”

“Shut it, Mori. It was my first time. Tsukino-se didn’t have enough people for sports…”

“Haha, that explains it… Hm?”

“What’s that?”

A sudden roar of cheers erupted from the gymnasium. With limited space, only half the students played while others spectated. Now, most spectators crowded the gym, piquing Hayato’s curiosity.

“Oh, the goddess of victory has descended.”

“Goddess?”

Urged by Mori, Hayato headed to the gym. There, he saw a goddess soaring—or, in his view, a monkey.

“How’s she even there?!”

“Her airtime’s insane, is she flying?!”

“Pass to Nikaidou-san!”

“She’s got three defenders on her, right?!”

It was a girls’ basketball game. One girl dominated, her dynamic play gripping the crowd. She darted across the court, never still, her superhuman stamina and agility toying with opponents like a monkey swinging between trees.

The game wasn’t just about Haruki. Cheers revealed the opposing team, though historically weak, was half basketball club members. Their skilled teamwork countered Haruki’s brilliance, creating a thrilling back-and-forth.

It was mesmerizing. And Hayato felt intense déjà vu.

(Show-off… She’s playing around.)

Haruki always added flair—whether gaming, jumping into rivers, or swinging sticks. Even now, while opponents panted, she looked unfazed, her cool demeanor drawing a frown from Hayato.

“Man, Nikaidou’s amazing, right, Kirishima?”

“Yeah… totally amazing, Mori.”

“Not just basketball, but that too.”

“That?”

Mori’s slack-jawed face matched other boys’. Following their gazes, Hayato landed on one point.

“!?”

“Pretty great, huh?”

“W-Well, uh…”

It was Haruki’s chest. Her breasts.

Not particularly large, but her relentless court movement— unmatched by others—made them bounce vertically, sway side-to-side, and arc dramatically with feints.

“It’s practically art,” Mori whispered, swallowing hard.

Though average-sized, their lively motion was undeniably captivating.

(No way, it’s Haruki!)

Hayato scolded himself, but as a teenage boy, once noticed, he couldn’t stop glancing. Then, after scoring, Haruki caught his gaze, flashing a smug “How’s that?” grin at the perfect moment.

(She saw me!? No, she didn’t, right!?)

Conflicting thoughts swirled, his face burning. He couldn’t stay.

“Where you going, Kirishima?”

“Uh, I’m a country boy, crowds make me dizzy.”

“Too bad.”

“Haha…”

Stumbling out, head spinning from heat, he shoved it under a faucet, twisting it on. Cheers mixed with water, stirring irritation.

“Damn it!”

Haruki was messing with him. He doused himself in frustration. Plenty of things he wasn’t used to yet.

Lunchtime, at the secret base.

Haruki bragged about her basketball performance, smirking.

“How was I? Pretty impressive, right?”

“…Sure.”

Hayato sulked, barely listening. Unbeknownst to him, Haruki had watched him flounder at soccer, falling for every trick. Assuming his mood was competitive sour grapes, she grew cockier.

But Hayato’s issue was different. Recalling the game made him hyper-aware of Haruki as a girl, stirring unease.

“So?”

“So?”

“How’d the game end? I didn’t stay.”

“Close, but we lost. Who’d you root for?”

“…”

“…”

Her teasing question and mischievous grin clearly toyed with him. Flushed from gym, she’d shed socks and her summer knit, fanning herself with an unbuttoned blouse—an inadvertently provocative yet sloppy sight, reserved for Hayato.

(Typical Haruki.)

The thought made his fixation feel silly, easing his frown.

“Hey, you listening?”

“Yeah, yeah, you win. I’m no match for you.”

“Finally admitting it? That’s a debt to me, right?”

“For what?”

“Who hyped the crowd more?”

“Hyped… You’re one hell of an actor.”

“…Actor, huh?”

Haruki’s vibe shifted abruptly. Her playful lightness vanished, replaced by a heavy air. Her smile masked pain, distressing to witness.

Hayato didn’t know what triggered it but realized he’d hit a landmine, panicking.

“…You and I, we’re totally different, huh?”

“W-Wait, Haruki!?”

Her smile morphed, and she crawled toward him like a predator, placing a hand on his chest, fingers gliding sensually as if testing something.

“Your chest… so hard. Muscles? Training? Or just being a guy? We used to be so similar.”

“S-Stop it, Haruki…!”

“Why?”

“There’s no why…!”

Her soft, deliberate fingers traced his chest, sometimes slipping under his shirt to graze skin. The unfamiliar sensation overwhelmed him.

“It tickles, stop!”

“Eep!”

Yanking her off, Hayato glared, teary-eyed. Haruki blinked, then burst out laughing.

“Haha, sorry! That ticklish?”

“Give me a break.”

“But, like… what do you think of me acting, fooling everyone?”

“Just think it’s you being you.”

“…Got it.”

Narrowing her eyes, she ended the topic, pulling out lunch—a jelly drink and an onigiri, alternating with sandwiches lately. Hayato followed, unpacking his bento.

“Here, as an apology, want a bite? Jelly or onigiri?”

“Nah, I’ve got mine. You always have those drinks, huh?”

“Quick nutrition.”

“Convenience store?”

“Yup, every morning. You always bring bento… Whoa, what’s that!?”

“What… Rice croquettes.”

Four fist-sized rice croquettes dominated his bento, no side dishes, shocking Haruki. But they looked filling. Prepped for gym day, he’d sautéed diced onions, eggplant, and bacon, mixed with leftover rice, seasoned with salt, pepper, and ketchup, shaped with wrap, and stuffed with cheese. Coated in flour, egg, and breadcrumbs, they were fried in a shallow pan.

Himeko had scolded, “Fried rice? The calories! Trying to fatten me up!?” but sneaked three anyway.

“Trade half your onigiri?”

“Really!?”

“Here.”

“Me too.”

Placing a croquette on his lid, Haruki stuffed an onigiri in its place.

“Chopsticks?”

“Nah, hands… Mmm, tasty! Bold flavor’s perfect post-gym, and the cheese! Store-bought? Frozen?”

“I made it.”

“You!?”

“What, surprised?”

“Yeah…”

Haruki studied his face, eyes skeptical.

“All your bentos too?”

“Yup, me.”

“…Seven years is a long time, huh?”

“Haruki—”

Her troubled smile stopped his words, breath catching.

“Let’s eat, lunch is almost over.”

“Right…”

Her playful grin returned instantly, but something lingered. Hayato glanced outside, distracted. The early summer sky was infuriatingly blue.

Since moving, Hayato struggled with new experiences, but some things, it seemed, he’d never get used to.

That afternoon, Haruki was caught by a gaggle of chattering girls.

“Hey, you watching Ten Years of Solitude?”

“Totally! Takura Mao’s our parents’ age, but looks like our sister!”

“She does… Oh, they’re filming nearby, right?”

“No way!? Oh, I heard Tsurumi from our class was talking to Kanemiya from D about the shoot, maybe inviting him!”

“Wait! They’ve been close lately, is it that!?”

“Whoa! What do you think, Nikaidou-san?”

“Uh, well, I…”

They’d veered from drama talk to gossip about classmates’ relationships. Not unusual, but Haruki seemed off. She smiled and nodded, but looked uncomfortable.

Hayato, chuckling, thought, She’s bad at this stuff. But her pale face caught him off guard—not sick, just… off. Only he’d notice such a subtle shift.

He couldn’t ignore it.

“Nikaidou, someone’s calling you from the hall.”

“…Huh?”

“They pointed toward the storage area and rushed off. Better hurry.”

“…Oh, right!”

With a knowing wink, Hayato signaled her. Getting it, Haruki grabbed her things and stood.

“Sorry, I’ve got something to do!”

As a model student, requests weren’t uncommon. The girls waved her off casually—“See ya!” “Good luck!”—and Hayato followed to their secret base in the old school building.

“Thanks, Hayato.”

“No big deal.”

Arriving later, he found Haruki slumped against the wall, knees hugged, clearly having read his intent. She patted the floor beside her. Having come as “help,” she couldn’t leave immediately.

Reluctant to leave her looking so vulnerable, Hayato sat. As he did, she sighed dramatically.

“Ugh, why do girls love talking about who’s crushing on who…?”

“Cause they’re girls? Himeko’s glued to those shows too.”

“Haha, her too? I’d rather talk about which character fits which mech, or which game producer’s hiring or team’s acting shady, not who’s dating who…”

“And? No one’s into that?”

“Haha, mostly guys, right? Back in middle school, I gave someone the wrong idea, and…”

“…Pfft!”

“Hayato!? It’s not funny! I’ve had to be careful who I talk to since!”

“Sorry, ow, stop hitting my back! …Must be tough, being so popular.”

“…It is.”

Burying her face in her knees, her voice darkened. Her serious tone silenced Hayato’s teasing.

Her voice grew bitter, laced with resentment.

“I’m bad at romance talk.”

“…”

A murmur, not meant for anyone, it was raw emotion, shaping who she was now. Her lonely expression urged Hayato to respond, but seven years’ fog blocked his words.

He could’ve brushed it off with “Got it.” But her lost look echoed Himeko’s from that time. Impulsively, he ruffled her hair roughly.

“Whoa! Hayato, what the heck!?”

“…Sorry. It usually works on Himeko, so…”

“Ugh, my hair’s a mess! This takes forever to set!”

“My bad.”

“…!”

As he pulled back at her protest, Haruki let out a soft, wistful sound, looking up at him.

“I’m not Himeko, you know. You can’t just brush me off…”

“Uh, well…”

Their eyes locked, her upward gaze unintended, a product of their grown differences. Her large, childhood-reminiscent eyes, full lips, and breath— undeniably pretty even without bias—startled him.

He looked away, but her delicate shoulders and modest curves asserted her femininity, forcing awareness. His throat tightened.

(Is Haruki… cute?)

Their gazes tangled. Knowing it was improper, he reached out again—when:

“What’s even out here?”

“I, uh, have a favor to ask, senpai.”

“!?”

They froze. Voices—a guy and girl—came from outside.

“Jeez, what do you want now? The manga’s latest volume’s out, no dice. I’m broke, and… oh, you still have my game—”

“Please lend me your life, forever…!”

“My… what!?”

“Mmph, mm… mmm…”

“Mmph… Pwah! W-Wait, what was that!? Your teeth hit me!”

“S-Sorry! It’s my first time!”

“W-Well, mine too, but…”

“Oh, it was your first too, good… I, um, I like you…”

“…! Uh, well… You’re always so sudden…”

“Senpai…”

“…Mm.”

The old school building, a storage area, was deserted—a perfect confession spot.

“…”

Blushing, Hayato and Haruki held their breath, overhearing the intimate exchange, comparing it to themselves. The air grew painfully awkward.

“Hehe, senpai!”

“Hey, you’re making it hard to walk!”

As the couple left, Hayato and Haruki sprang apart, looking away.

“Uh, that was, uh, that thing!”

“Y-Yeah, that, totally that!”

The couple’s vibe left them fidgety. Pointlessly, they rummaged through their bags, repacking meticulously.

After calming down, Haruki murmured.

“…I’m really bad at romance talk.”

“Same here.”

They exchanged wry smiles.
