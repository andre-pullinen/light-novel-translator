Episode 14: She’s just being nostalgic in front of me, right…? No, wait a minute!
The rain that poured all night stopped by dawn. The sky, cleansed of dust, shone a deep, clear blue. The assertive morning sun pierced through the curtains, rousing Haruki’s eyelids despite the early hour.

“Nn… ugh… huh?”

Waking to an unfamiliar room, Haruki felt disoriented. Her groggy mind struggled, but her heart was oddly calm. As she scanned her surroundings, clarity returned.

“Oh, right. I slept over.”

She was in Hayato’s room. He’d insisted she take the bed, dismissing her offer to take the sofa. The clock read just before 5 a.m. Despite waking early, she felt no drowsiness—proof of a deep, restful sleep.

It was a strange sensation. The lingering weight in her heart, like sediment, was gone, washed away with last night’s tears. Her mood was as clear and bright as the sky.

“I can at least be by your side.”

Hayato’s words from last night echoed in her mind. The unfamiliar scent of his shirt and bedding enveloped her. Clutching the oversized shirt, its hem as long as her summer dress, she felt a warmth like being embraced.

“…Hehe.”

A laugh bubbled up naturally. Wrapped in the shirt, she felt secure, aware of her own giddiness. Embarrassed yet happy, she buried her face in the pillow, squealing “Mya! Mya!” and rolling across the bed.

(There’s a promise to be protected, too…)

She gazed at her left pinky, recalling the sensation of their promise. Hayato’s face flashed in her mind, stirring restless feelings. Confused by her emotions, she shook her head to clear the haze.

“Anyway, it’s Monday. No time to linger.”

School awaited. Though her assignments were done, she needed to return home to change into her uniform and take out the trash. Trying to stay composed, she tiptoed to the living room.

“Nn… ugh…”
“—!”

There lay Hayato, a thin blanket barely covering his stomach. He slept deeply, oblivious, one leg dangling off the sofa, snoring carefree. His messy sleeping posture contrasted his peaceful face.

Seeing him, Haruki’s chest flared with a sudden, intense heartbeat, like embers igniting.

(What’s this…)

Confused by the unfamiliar emotion, she realized it wasn’t new. Since reuniting with Hayato, her feelings had been in constant turmoil. On her first day as a transfer student, she wondered how he’d see her changed appearance, only for him to tease her with the same old smirk, calling her a monkey youkai. Unlike other boys, he offered help without ulterior motives. Her attempts to tease him often backfired, leaving her flustered. Last night, she’d bared her vulnerabilities completely.

Recalling it made her face burn with shame, yet Hayato slept on, blissfully unaware.

(It’s unfair that I’m the only one feeling like this…!)

Thanks to him, her heart felt lighter. He’d saved her, likely because of their childhood oath to always be friends. While grateful, she felt frustrated at being the only one affected, always on the receiving end.

Childishly, she decided to prank him. With a mischievous grin, she approached.

(Let’s see what I can do.)

She studied him closely: messy, stiff hair; surprisingly long lashes; slightly tanned skin; a face reminiscent of Himeko’s yet distinctly masculine, stirring an unfamiliar attraction.

(Wait, is Hayato… kinda handsome?)

The sudden thought startled her. To her, he’d always been just Hayato. Her heart pounded painfully, and her gaze fell on his dry, cracked lips. They seemed almost… enticing. Absurdly, she wanted to moisten them. Drawn in, her face inched closer.

“Nn…”
“—!?”

Hayato rolled over abruptly. Nearly colliding, Haruki snapped back, pressing a hand to her lips. Her heart raced as she glanced at him, still sleeping soundly, oblivious.

(What was I…)

It was unconscious, but crossing a line no friend should. Shocked and confused, her mind was consumed by the infuriatingly calm Hayato.

(I can’t let this slide! I’m always the one losing!)

Burning with childish defiance, desperate to even the score and face him without embarrassment, she returned to his room and opened his closet.

“Wow, so organized… then it’s probably… bingo!”

Finding what she wanted, she changed and returned to the living room. It was 5 a.m., and Hayato still slept. Testing his deep sleep, she poked his cheek.

“Hey, you awake? If you don’t wake up, something big’s gonna happen! You sure?”

Whispering softly, she got only a goofy “Nn—” in response.

(He’s always been impossible to wake once he’s out.)

Confident he wouldn’t stir, she boldly repositioned him.

“This is payback, got it?”

Muttering as if to justify herself, she snapped a photo with her phone, the shutter sound echoing in the quiet room. A childish, mischievous grin spread across her face.

“Nn… huh? Oh, right…”

Hayato woke at his usual time, in an unfamiliar setting, feeling aches from sleeping on the sofa. Stretching, he noticed a note on the dining table.

“Thanks for yesterday. Gotta go back to change. Forget last night, okay!?”

The forceful handwriting betrayed Haruki’s emotions. Beside it was the neatly folded T-shirt she’d worn, faintly carrying her sweet scent.

“She could’ve stayed for breakfast,” he mumbled, embarrassed by the lingering fragrance. Without proof, he felt Haruki was okay now, and he sighed in relief.

“Time to get moving.”

Monday mornings were busy and mildly depressing. Trash, school prep, breakfast, and bentos kept him occupied, helping him avoid cringing at the embarrassing lines he’d said last night.

“Yawn… morning, Onii. Where’s Haru-chan?”
“Had stuff to do, so she left. Go wash your face, Himeko. Your bedhead’s wild. I’ll get breakfast ready.”
“Ugh, it’s exploding… huh? Onii, what’s with your neck?”
“Neck? Oh, I slept wrong—”
“Not that. The right side’s red.”
“Right? It’s the left that hurts… Himeko?”

As he cooked, Himeko tugged his shirt from behind. Turning, he saw her lonely yet forced smile.

“…Is Haru-chan okay?”
“Himeko…”

She must’ve sensed something from last night, possibly from their phone chats. Like Hayato, the seven-year gap made her hesitate to pry. But he knew one thing for sure.

“She’ll be fine.”
“Why?”
“‘Cause I—her friend—am by her side.”
“…Pfft! Cheesy!”
“Shut up!”

Himeko blinked, then burst out laughing, her face brightening. Hayato grinned, caught up in her mood.

“Hehe… guess I gotta step up too.”

The rain-cleared sky was bluer than ever. The early summer sun scorched Hayato’s skin on his way to school.

“Hot… but post-rain, huh…”

Rain, a blessing, spurred crop growth but could damage furrows or burst fruits. Thinking of a recent promise, he headed to the school garden.

“Morning! Looks like a good harvest?”
“Oh, Kirishima-san!”

There, Minamo Mitake, a petite girl with distinctive curly hair, busily harvested vegetables. The rain and morning sun had yielded a bountiful crop, filling two cardboard boxes. She’d clearly been tending the garden since early.

“Was gonna offer help, but looks like you’re done.”
“Thanks to the weather, we got a lot! But, um…”
“Too much to handle?”
“Yes…”

Minamo smiled sheepishly. Even split with Hayato, the vegetables—especially eggplants—were excessive. Bumper crop poverty crossed his mind; in rural Tsukino-se, overabundance often led to forced sharing.

“Tomatoes I can share with Grandpa, but eggplants…”
“Try pickling them overnight. Where I used to live, they were great with tea or sake.”
“How do you make that?”
“I’ll record the recipe and send it.”
“Oh! You got a phone!”
“Yesterday.”

They exchanged IDs. Hayato, nervous about asking a new acquaintance, especially a girl, felt relieved at keeping his promise. But Minamo, blushing and apologetic, caught his attention.

“Um, was it… okay to exchange with me?”
“Huh? What do you—”
“Like, your girlfriend or…”
“Girlfriend?”

Confused, Hayato followed her gaze to his neck, where Himeko had pointed earlier.

“Oh, this? Himeko mentioned it. Is it that noticeable?”
“It’s great to be close! But a kiss mark… hauu!”
“Mitake-san!?”

Misunderstanding wildly, Minamo fled, face red. Left with the vegetables, Hayato sent his first phone message: “Holding the veggies. Neck’s just a bug bite.” A lame excuse.

Nearing the classroom, Hayato’s thoughts fixated on Haruki. His childhood friend, with whom he’d scraped knees and dirtied clothes, stacking memories and swearing eternal friendship. Yet, upon reuniting, she was unrecognizable.

Their heights, once equal, now differed by a head. Their clasped hands, once similar, were a size apart. Though their running speed matched, their strides didn’t. Her short hair was now long and glossy, her scarred skin now flawless, her summer dress too stylish to soil.

Her charm flustered him, a natural reaction to her transformation into a captivating girl. Yet, playing together felt as fun as ever, their bond unchanged. Even if he was a plain transfer student and she a popular beauty, that didn’t matter.

“Morning… huh?”
“Yo, Kirishima! What’s with your neck?”
“Apparently a kiss mark. Sadly, it’s from a bug… what’s that? Unusual.”
“Haha, yeah. First time I’ve seen Nikaido like that.”

Entering the classroom, Hayato saw Haruki surrounded at his desk, a typical sight. But today, she was actively leading the conversation.

“This is Hime-chan—we picked these out together, see, this too!”
“So cute!”
“You pull off all kinds of styles!”
“Some are obviously tacky, but it feels playful.”
“T-Tacky… haha…”

Nikaido Haruki was a star, idol-like in looks and demeanor. People flocked to her, but she rarely initiated. Seeing her proudly show off her phone was striking—hard to ignore.

(She’s talking to others like that too…)

Watching her bubbly demeanor, Hayato felt an odd unease. A classmate explained she was showing photos with her childhood friend, shopping for clothes over the weekend. Hayato’s reaction was muted; he could picture Haruki’s quirky wardrobe, and the “friend” was his sister. He managed a wry smile.

Still, her eagerness to share about Himeko suggested a wall coming down, a positive change, likely sparked by last night’s tears. Convincing himself it was good, he headed to his seat.

“Morning, Nikaido-san.”
“Good morning, Kirishima-kun.”
“Popular as ever, huh?”
“Wanna see? It’s my childhood friend. Look, isn’t it well-shot?”

Haruki eagerly shoved her phone at him. Knowing her “friend” was Himeko, Hayato found it pointless—she knew he’d seen her plenty. Feeling the earlier unease, he responded curtly. Yet, Haruki persistently pushed the phone, as if proudly showing off her friend, a heartwarming sight to others.

“Nah, I’m good—wha!?”
“Yah!”

Seeing the screen, Hayato snatched the phone. Haruki’s cute yelp echoed, and his sudden move drew wide-eyed stares. He was too shocked to care about the class’s assumptions.

“W-Wait, you, this…!”
“Pretty good, right? My precious childhood friend.”

The photo showed Haruki in Hayato’s uniform shirt, arm around him, lips near his neck in a selfie. A cheeky caption read, “Hayato’s sleeping next to me.” The mark on his neck was clearly her doing. She smiled—not her usual refined smile, but the mischievous grin from their childhood.

Thoughts raced. He didn’t know what happened in those seven years, likely something she couldn’t yet share. But he trusted she’d tell him when the gap closed.

“Haruki, we’ll always be friends!”



No need to rush. Their childhood promise still held. Yet, Hayato wanted to shout.

“Ha-Har… Nikaido…!”
“Hehe!”

—She’s just being nostalgic in front of me, right…? No, wait a minute!
