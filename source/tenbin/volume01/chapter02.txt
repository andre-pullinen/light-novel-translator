Episode 2: The Promise
“Argh, I overslept!”

Himeko’s piercing scream echoed through the Kirishima household at dawn, far from refined.

Hayato muttered, “Again…” with an exasperated look, deftly flipping eggs in a rectangular tamagoyaki pan. He was making dashimaki tamago, a savory rolled omelet infused with bonito—a specialty endorsed at village feasts. Chopped mizuna from fridge cleanup added texture he liked.

“Why didn’t you wake me?!”

“Look at the clock.”

“It’s past 7:30!”

“Plenty of time.”

“Even sprinting… oh, right.”

Himeko, bedhead intact, stuck out her tongue sheepishly. The sudden move had its downsides, but a shorter commute was a clear win.

“What’s that, Onii?”

“Bento. Leftovers plus dashimaki. Yesterday’s cafeteria chaos was… intense.”

“Yeah, got it. So, dear brother?”

“Got yours too.”

“Knew you’d come through!”

The cafeteria mob had chilled Hayato. Students charging for food resembled a battlefield. City kids might be used to it, but rural Hayato wasn’t trained for daily combat. Assuming Himeko felt the same, he’d prepped for both.

Hayato was a bit of a caretaker.

Time was tight but not leisurely. They ate, prepped, and left together, locking the door.

“Hot…”

“Sweltering…”

Stepping outside, the siblings groaned in unison. Unlike the countryside’s bare earth, asphalt trapped heat. With no trees to shield them, early summer sun scorched their skin. This city felt hotter than Tsukino-se, dampening their mood as they walked to school.

“See ya, I’m this way.”

“Later.”

Parting with Himeko, Hayato missed rural coolness. The crowds and lack of greenery screamed new world. Unwanted, it’d take time to adjust.

(Oh, right.)

Thinking of home sparked a memory: the school’s backyard garden, reminiscent of Tsukino-se. Zucchini flowers bloomed mornings, wilting by afternoon, needing early pollination. He pictured the frantic girl tending them.

(Is she okay?)

Passing the school gate, his feet carried him to the garden.

The spot, though behind the school, got good sun and little foot traffic—ideal for plants. From afar, zucchini’s large yellow flowers bloomed. The curly-haired girl, like Gen-jiisan’s sheep, fretted before them.

“Morning. What’s up?”

“Eek! …Oh, you’re from yesterday.”

“Flowers are blooming. Pollinated yet?”

“Uh, well…”

“Cotton swabs make it easier.”

“…Don’t have any.”

Blushing, she looked down, clearly underprepared. Without action, the zucchinis wouldn’t fruit. Hayato wasn’t callous enough to walk away after speaking up.

“See, female flowers have a green fruit base; males don’t. Can I pick one?”

“Huh? Y-Yes, please!”

“Petals get in the way, so strip them… then rub the stamen on the pistil. Got it?”

“I’ll try! Like this… uh…”

“Don’t use all the pollen at once. One stamen can do two or three pistils.”

“Okay!”

Following his advice, she started pollinating. The garden was small but sizable for a flowerbed. With little time before homeroom, Hayato hurried, his heart lifting at the familiar farm work, a smile creeping in.

“I thought veggies just grew on their own…”

“Hm?”

“Stamens and pistils coming together, that process makes fruit… These plants are alive, and we eat them…”

“Yeah… you’re right.”

To Hayato, farm work was routine in agrarian Tsukino-se, just a task. Her perspective felt fresh, drawing his gaze. Noticing, she flushed, flailing her hands in panic.

“W-Wait, that’s weird, right?! Stamens and pistils sound… naughty… ugh!”

“Whoa, hold on!”

“N-No, I mean, stamens and pistils are like making babies—kyaa!”

“Calm down!”

Her sudden outburst stumped him. Lacking experience with girls his age, he floundered.

“Stamens, pistils, and a blushing Mitake-san… What’s going on, Kirishima-kun?”

“N-Nikaidou-san!”

“Haru… Nikaidou!”

Haruki appeared, her narrowed eyes accusatory. Her beauty carried an odd intensity, making Hayato and Mitake step back.

“Uh, I-I… Good morning, excuse me!”

“…Oh.”

Overwhelmed, Mitake fled like a startled rabbit. Hayato was left alone with a sulky Haruki.

“Look, it’s just—”

“Heh, she really does look like Gen-san’s scolded sheep, huh, Hayato?”

“…Haruki?”

Expecting to explain, her playful tone surprised him. Her grin was pure mischief, like a kid pulling a prank.

“How long were you watching?”

“Since halfway through pollination? I wondered what you were doing, then she freaked out, so I figured I’d bail you out.”

“That’s a while! Could’ve said something… Now she thinks I said something weird.”

“I’ve got a school persona to maintain, you know.”

“And I don’t?”

“You’re fine.”

Twirling her skirt, Haruki grinned.

“We’re friends, after all.”

“What’s that logic?”

Her reasoning was absurd, but their stifled giggles filled the air.

(Guess it’s fine.)

Somehow, Hayato let it slide. They headed to class together, time pressing. Far from the entrance, they hurried shoulder-to-shoulder, like old times.

Feeling her gaze, he glanced over.

“What?”

“Nothing~ Just not thinking about how I used to look down at you~”

“Such a kid!”

“Hmph!”

Pouting, she muttered, “I was taller back then.” Her Haruki-like antics drew a fond, exasperated sigh from Hayato. Same old childhood friend, sharing memories. But in class, they became a dull transfer student and an untouchable idol.

“There’s Nikaidou-san.”

“Let’s ask her. About the English homework translation…”

“Sorry, me too.”

“Me three!”

“Uh, me? Sure, okay.”

Donning her mask, Haruki was instantly swarmed, desks crowding around her. They sought homework help, though some just wanted to chat.

(She’s top-tier, right?)

Recalling yesterday’s talk, Hayato figured many just craved her attention. She likely knew, yet responded gracefully, her popularity undeniable.

Though next to her, Hayato, averse to crowds, retreated to the window, observing his popular friend.

(Her “disguise,” huh?)

He recalled her term. He’d been fooled too, but didn’t mind. Haruki was Haruki. Her disguise had reasons he trusted she’d share if needed. For now, he watched her mobbed state with a wry smile.

“Nikaidou’s crazy popular, huh? It’s always like that.”

“Impressive. She’s cute, sure, but… uh?”

“Oh, intros. I’m Mori, Iori Mori. Nice to meet ya, transfer—er, Kirishima.”

“Yo, Mori. Likewise.”

A bleached-blond, laid-back guy—one of yesterday’s questioners—joined him, eyeing Haruki.

“Jumping into that crowd’s tough for a newbie.”

“I’m not into that. You’re not joining?”

“She’s out of reach. Got a girlfriend anyway, so I just admire.”

“Got it.”

“Plenty of guys do the same.”

“Really?”

Scanning the room, some chatted, copied homework, or read, occasionally glancing at Haruki but not obsessed. She was special, her world distant. Hayato felt the same—or should. Yet, watching her, his brow furrowed.

“…”

“Ha, go for it, Kirishima.”

“Huh? Go for what?”

“You know.”

“No, you’re misunderstanding!”

“Haha!”

Mori teased, sensing his mood. Seven years was long; they’d grown apart. They weren’t kids anymore.

(Maybe we shouldn’t interact at school.)

Gorgeous, talented, popular—Haruki was an idol. Her “disguise” had purpose. Supporting that was his role as her friend.

“Sigh…”

“Kirishima?”

“Nothing.”

“You sure?”

A tinge of loneliness hit, but Hayato exhaled, watching her.

Lunchtime arrived. Haruki had been mobbed all morning, highlighting their different worlds.

“Kirishima-kun, come with me.”

“Huh… Nikaidou-san?”

Her words stunned him. Her calm smile held a desperate edge, impossible to ignore.

The classroom buzzed. As an idol, her actions drew eyes. She knew her role and its weight. Her approaching a boy casually was momentous, sparking whispers.

“Nikaidou-san with the transfer?”

“Maybe she likes him…”

“No, it’s just transfer student business, please!”

Curious and jealous gazes followed. Too late to undo, they both knew it.

“Uh, it’s, um, that. That thing.”

“That…? Nikaidou?”

Coolly repeating “that,” she was incomprehensible. To others, Hayato seemed clueless, but he saw her panic—realizing her mistake, covering frantically.

(Oh, right.)

A memory surfaced. As kids, Haruki, cocky, walked a fence between a pasture and field, breaking it. No sheep escaped, thanks to nearby farmers, and the rotten fence wasn’t her fault. But she’d panicked, chanting, “It’s that, that thing, that’s how it went…” Now, her composed face and pleading eyes mirrored that moment.

(Tch.)

Stifling a laugh, Hayato chose his words.

“Oh, that. The garden thing I was asked about this morning.”

“Yes! That’s it. I want to wrap it up… You free now?”

“Got it.”

“Bring your bag too, please.”

“Sure thing.”

His quick improv shifted the narrative to “finishing a task.” The class relaxed—“Oh, that’s all?”—losing interest. Haruki’s relief was blatant as she hurried out. Hayato sighed, met by Mori’s grin.

“Lucky break, transfer.”

“Shut it.”

They reached a small, barren room in the old school building, a quarter of a classroom’s size. Worn wooden floors, spotless from care, hinted at history.

“What’s this?”

“My secret base. This area’s just storage, so no one comes.”

“A base with nothing?”

“Haha, true. I’ll bring stuff next time. It’s also a shelter.”

“Shelter, huh.”

Alone, Haruki reverted to her tomboy self, plopping down cross-legged, skirt ignored. She hesitated to remove her socks, though.

(Can’t let the class see this.)

Hayato rubbed his temple, scanning the room. Bare, it was a lonely hideout, a refuge from chaos, just a windowed empty space.

“What’s with this place?”

“Found it by chance. Got a key too.”

“That allowed?”

“No one’ll know. Sit.”

“Ugh.”

Sitting cross-legged opposite her, he pressed.

“So, what’s the deal?”

“Well… it’s kinda…”

She hesitated, mumbling. Her impulsive invite, despite her facade, carried weight. Her earnest eyes suggested something urgent.

“You won’t laugh?”

“Depends.”

“Laugh, and it’s a debt.”

“Fine.”

Her serious gaze met his. He faced her squarely.

“Truth is… I’ve always dreamed of eating lunch with a friend.”

“…Huh?”

A dumb sound escaped him. Mistaking it for scorn, she bristled.

“Hey! It’s a big deal to me! I’m, you know, like that… Eating with someone caused drama before, so I’ve always been alone…”

Her voice faded.

Her words painted a clear picture: the classroom frenzy, this “shelter.” She’d spent lunches alone here, a thought that stung.

(Damn it…)

Scratching his head to mask the ache, Hayato pulled out his bento.

“Guess your dream’s coming true daily now.”

“Hayato…”

“What, I’m wrong?”

“No, you’re right. This is my debt to you!”

“Cheap debt.”

“Haha, ten lunches for one debt, then.”

“That’ll just pile up your debts. How’s this: no special plans, we meet here for lunch. Deal?”

“Deal… A promise… Yeah, a promise, Hayato!”

“Whoa, okay.”

Blinking, her face lit up with a childlike grin. Overwhelmed, she leaned in close, foreheads nearly touching.

(Too close!)

Her beauty, undeniable, and her rare, unguarded smile so near sent his heart racing. Embarrassed, he didn’t want her to notice.

“Back off.”

“Oh, sorry!”

Pushing her away roughly, he stuck out his pinky.

“A promise.”

“Yup, promise. Hehe.”

Linked pinkies sealed their small, secret vow, laughter spilling out. Another memory, like old times, was born between them.
