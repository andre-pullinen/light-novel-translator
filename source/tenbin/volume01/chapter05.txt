Episode 5: Hayato, You Idiot!
One day after school, at the nearby supermarket.

Hayato Kirishima heard a now-familiar bell-like voice.

“Oh.”

“…Yo.”

His reply was awkward, and no wonder—he was caught in an embarrassing act. Crouched in the stationery and toy aisle, he was intently rummaging through items.

“Collectible toys?”

“No, it’s just…”

He was thoroughly inspecting them—picking each up, shaking to check contents, pressing boxes to guess what’s inside. Utterly serious.

Haruki Nikaidou wouldn’t miss this. Grinning like a kid with a new toy, she watched as Hayato panicked.

“Fancy meeting you here! Running into you outside school feels weird. Still in uniform, so you’re heading home?”

“Hm, dinosaur fossils and gemstones… Oh yeah, you loved collecting odd rocks.”

“Right, I was in the middle of shopping! Gotta finish and head back!”

“I made a Showa-era candy store diorama with these.”

“…Seriously?”

“Yup, look.”

Haruki showed her phone. A ramshackle wooden shop with a cola sign, stocked with retro candies, a vending machine, gacha, ice cream freezer, and even a bug net—pure Showa nostalgia.

“That’s Grandma Murao’s shop!”

“Right? Built it from memory.”

“Amazing, even the ground and trees… How?”

“Dollar store. Boards, clay, colored powder, glue, tape, brushes—everything’s there.”

“No way, dollar stores! They’re real, not urban legends!”

“There’s one at the station… Wait, that’s what shocks you!?”

“Well… but you really made this with toys…”

“It was tough. I wanted the ice cream freezer, but kept getting benches… Ugh, I questioned the meaning of probability…”

“Uh, Haruki-san…?”

Haruki’s eyes lost their spark, her expression vacant yet smiling—clearly unhinged. Hayato instinctively sensed a swamp pulling him in, a chill running down his spine. He quietly returned the toy to the shelf.

“Not buying? This rural festival stall series looks tempting.”

“Nope, shopping! Back to shopping, Haruki!”

“Nooo, the toys!”

Forcibly dragging the swamp dweller Haruki away, Hayato learned: collectible toys are a trap.

After rushed shopping, they left, unusually exhausted.

“Phew, that was close… I nearly dove into a new swamp…”

“Keep it moderate… Hm?”

Hayato’s load was light: chicken, konjac, burdock for tonight’s chikuzenni, plus salmon fillets. A kid could carry it.

Haruki, however, was overloaded, struggling to juggle bags.

So, it was natural for Hayato.

“Here, gimme those—oof, heavy. This too.”

“Hayato!?”

“What? Carry the rest.”

“Oh… okay.”

“It’s mostly frozen. Let’s hurry.”

Half-forcing, he took her bags, urging her home. Haruki was stunned and confused.

At school, others offered to carry her things, always with ulterior motives. Hayato’s help—purely because she was struggling—was new. In Tsukino-se, ignoring someone in need would make you village gossip fodder. It was just habit.

But to Haruki, it wasn’t.

Her heart swirled with surprise, joy, confusion, and questions, forcing her to notice Hayato.

“You always buy this much?”

“Uh, yeah. I’m a bulk buyer.”

“Got it.”

“Yeah…”

“…”

“…”

Silence fell. They walked side-by-side as always, now with a head’s height difference. Haruki stole glances at his face, but Hayato strode on, his usual profile somehow irking her.

She wanted to speak but also didn’t, her complex feelings lingering as they walked at dusk. Only their footsteps sounded, oddly comforting.

(Guess that’s just Hayato.)

They reached her unlit, empty home. For seven years, no one welcomed her to this dark house. Returning with Hayato felt strange.

“We’re here. Where do I put these?”

“I’ll unlock the door, just leave them at the entrance.”

“Got it… I’m off.”

“Wait!”

“Hm?”

Her words were impulsive, uncalculated, raw with honesty, unsettling her.

“Number! Right, your phone number or SNS ID! We haven’t exchanged contacts!”

“Oh… sorry.”

“…What?”

Her rushed words met a reply that blanked her mind. Rejection was unthinkable. Her brain refused to process it, buried loneliness surfacing, her heart straining painfully. Feeling something vital slip away, she—

“I don’t actually have a smartphone…”

“Hayato, you IDIOT!!”

Her scream, born of relief, rang out louder than ever.
