Now, in the present.

Hayato Kirishima stood not in the rural mountains of Tsukino-se but before a massive urban building, hours away by car.

“So big…” he sighed, awestruck by the high school.

Compared to the leaky, wooden single-story school back home, this three-story white concrete structure was grand and pristine, almost dizzying. Overwhelmed, Hayato recalled nostalgic memories to escape reality.

Shaking it off, he headed to the staff room. Formalities were already done, and his homeroom teacher led him to his new classroom, marked “1-A” above the door.

As it opened, curious stares pierced him, making his shoulders twitch. Understandably—more students filled this room than his entire rural school.

“I’m Hayato Kirishima,” he said. “From Tsukino-se, where you’d bump into monkeys, deer, or boars on the road. I’m a country bumpkin, so… teach me the ropes, maybe?”

His self-deprecating introduction earned friendly chuckles, not a roaring hit but warm enough. After days of practice, Hayato sighed in relief.

Starting mid-June in a new city was daunting, but exciting too. He’d moved to the same city as Haruki Nikaidou, his childhood friend with whom he’d made a promise. The memory of young him grinning flashed in his mind.

“Your seat… Nikaidou’s neighbor is free,” the teacher said.

“Nikaidou—huh?” Hayato blinked.

“Yes,” a girl raised her hand, signaling his spot.

She was stunning.

Large, bright eyes, neatly braided hair, and a smile like a blooming bud—gentle, refined, the epitome of a Yamato Nadeshiko. A rare beauty, unlike any in Tsukino-se.

Hayato’s heart skipped, but he thought, Oh, she’s a Nikaidou too, like that guy. Imagining his mischievous old friend, he mused, If Haruki were here, he’d probably tease her about their shared surname, annoying her.

A chuckle escaped. “Nice to meet you, Nikaidou-san.”

She blinked, surprised, then flashed a playful grin. “Likewise, Kirishima-kun.”



(…Huh?)

Her narrowed eyes stirred a nostalgic feeling. Why does this feel familiar? Hayato tilted his head, but classmates swarmed, bombarding the new transfer student with questions.

“Is that stuff you said for real?”

“Deer and monkeys on the road? Seriously?”

“Why’d you move here?”

“My dad’s sudden job transfer,” Hayato explained, shrugging. “Tsukino-se had four buses a day, more livestock than people. I’m not used to being surrounded like this—chickens and sheep don’t count.”

Laughter erupted—“What’s that?” “No way!” “Hilarious!”—and the warm reception eased his nerves. Questions piled on.

“No girlfriend back there?”

“Hard to find anyone my age.”

“Friends? What’d you do for fun?”

“Mostly solo gaming or farm work… but I had one close friend. We’d jump into rivers from bridges, get stuck in trees, fall off… Less a friend, more a troublesome monkey or youkai—”

Recalling Haruki, Hayato smiled. Those chaotic memories were fun, loosening his lips now.

Crack!

“Huh?”

“…”

A sharp snap came from beside him. All eyes turned to Nikaidou, holding a broken mechanical pencil, looking startled herself.

A refined beauty and a snapped pencil—an odd pair. The class’s focus shifted from Hayato’s stories to her.

“Nikaidou-san?”

“How’d that happen?”

“You okay? Not hurt?”

“Ah, haha, I’m fine. Must’ve been defective,” she stammered, flustered, her awkward smile masking something. Her gaze, slightly accusing, turned to Hayato. “Calling an important friend such names is harsh, isn’t it?”

“Haha, only ‘cause they’re important,” he replied, thinking of Haruki.

“…Is that so?” She huffed, turning away.

◇◇◇

His response must’ve been a misstep. During class, Hayato sensed displeasure from the beauty beside him. Maybe he was imagining it, but her averted gazes suggested otherwise.

(What do I do…?)

Lessons marched on, differing from his old school. He focused to keep up, but needed help.

“Sorry, uh, last week’s handout?” he asked.

“…” Silence.

“Uh, I mean—”

“Here. Hard to see from there. Move your desk closer,” she said.

“Thanks.”

“No problem.”

Thankfully, she helped readily, not outright hating him—more like pouting. He couldn’t figure her out.

(If it were Himeko, I’d bribe her with sweets…)

From rural Tsukino-se, Hayato’s only peer reference was his sister. Another friend of Himeko’s existed, but she avoided him, so no help there. Treating Nikaidou like his sister was clearly wrong.

Deciding to ask directly, he steeled himself for the next break.

“Nikaidou—”

“Hey, Kirishima, more questions!”

“I’ve got some too!”

“About your old town—”

Classmates cut him off, eager for the novelty of a transfer student. Nikaidou sighed, exasperated, watching Hayato get swarmed.

Questions persisted every break, leaving no chance to talk until lunch. Classmates prioritized food, forming groups to eat. A perfect opportunity.

(Gotta talk to her.)

It might be trivial, but something nagged him. Plus, Nikaidou was undeniably cute—Hayato, a healthy teen, didn’t want to be disliked.

“Nikaidou-san—”

“Sorry, is Nikaidou-san here!?” a petite girl called.

“Yes, here,” Nikaidou replied, leaving.

Hayato’s outstretched hand hung empty. Some boys approached, grinning.

“Already eyeing Nikaidou? Bold, transfer kid. I get it.”

“She’s gorgeous, kind, smart, and athletic clubs want her.”

“Probably off for student council or club stuff now.”

“Not like that—but, wow, impressive.”

She was the quintessential honor student—beautiful, talented, humble. A manga-like figure, Hayato marveled. (Nothing like that Nikaidou… Well, different gender.) He smirked.

“She’s out of reach, man.”

“Super popular in middle school, but no dating rumors.”

“You got shut down hard, didn’t you?”

“Shut up! Don’t get your hopes up, Kirishima.”

“I’m not trying to—”

She was, predictably, a heartthrob. Hayato wasn’t chasing her; he just found her cute but confusing. Why was she, a stranger, so curt with him?

“Hm, no clue.”

Puzzled, his urge to talk grew.

Exhausted from constant attention, Hayato felt drained. (More people than a festival…) Declining a lunch invite, he explored, seeking the school store.

“Ugh…” The store was a chaotic peak-hour mess, worse than the classroom. He grabbed a margarine-filled katsu sandwich, sighing. (Bento from tomorrow.)

Wanting solitude, he wandered for a quiet spot, but found none. Behind the school, expecting emptiness, he spotted a girl—and something familiar.

“Hm?”

About to leave, he noticed vegetables—rare in the city—drawing his interest. The small, curly-haired girl tending them reminded him of something.

He approached, despite his usual reserve.

“Ugh, why won’t they fruit? Bad fertilizer…?” she muttered.

“Zucchini?” Hayato asked.

“Eek!?”



“Sorry for startling you. Those yellow flowers are zucchini, right? Purple ones are eggplants, white ones shishito peppers… Corn too?”

“Y-Yes, that’s right!”

It was a garden, bordered by bricks, with raised soil beds planted with vegetables. Hayato wasn’t one to chat up strangers, especially girls, but he couldn’t resist.

“Pollinating them? Zucchini needs pollen on female flowers to grow big.”

“Oh…!”

“Trim excess eggplant flowers, prune some shishito branches for more fruit.”

“Hauu…”

She frantically flipped through a notebook, blushing as she compared it to the garden. Hayato’s tips were basic rural knowledge, nothing boastworthy.

“You know a lot,” she said.

“Helped with fields back home. Gardening club?”

“Y-Yes, gardening club.”

“Vegetables in a gardening club?”

“Is… that weird?”

“Nah, it’s cool. Tomatoes were ornamental once. I like vegetable flowers.”

“!”

To Hayato, these harvest-signaling blooms were more familiar than florist flowers. (Field work earned pocket money too.) He chuckled.

Her surprised, flustered reaction—small and animal-like—further reminded him of something, softening his expression.

“What are you doing?” a bell-like voice, tinged with exasperation, came from behind.

“Mitake-san, the fertilizer you requested arrived at the club building,” said Nikaidou, her cold gaze fixed.

“Oh, yes! I’ll go, thank you, Nikaidou-san!” Mitake bolted.

“Uh, Nikaidou-san—”

Hands on hips, Nikaidou glared, leaning close. “Hmph, hitting on girls on your first day? Is that your type, Kirishima-kun?”

“N-No, it’s not—” Her stunning face and intensity made him step back.

Her casual tone and attitude, shedding her polite facade, baffled him further.

“Not hitting on her! She reminded me of someone…”

“Who?”

“…Gen-jii’s sheep.”

“Those sheep kept to eat weeds but loved vegetable seedlings, always getting scolded?”

“Yeah, her curly hair, scurrying around vegetables… I couldn’t help—ow!”

“Pfft… Haha!” Nikaidou burst out laughing, smacking his back.

“Calling her a sheep? You’re awful, Hayato.”

“Ow, easy—Haru… ki…?”

The name slipped out, uncertain. Why did he say it? Staring at her, confusion swirled.

“Nikaidou-san, over here!” another girl called.

“Yes, what is it?” Nikaidou replied, slipping back into her polite mask.

“Oi!”

“Shh.” She turned, pressing a finger to her lips with a mischievous grin, then left.

“What the heck…?”

A storm of thoughts raged in Hayato’s mind.

(Haruki…?)

During afternoon classes, he fixated on her—Haruki. His childhood friend from Tsukino-se’s mountains, running wild together.

“I caught one! Me too, Hayato!”

“I get it, stop hitting me!”

He recalled Haruki’s habit of smacking his back when excited. Her familiar banter triggered the name slip—etched deeply in his heart.

(Is Nikaidou-san… Haruki?)

Only a Tsukino-se local would know Gen-jii. Observing Nikaidou Haruki with narrowed eyes, he struggled to reconcile this refined girl with the monkey-like youkai of his memories.

“…Hmph!”

“!?”

Noticing his stare, she flicked a piece of eraser at him like a forehead flick. It didn’t hurt, but her childish act startled him.

(What a kid!)

Smirking at his reaction, Haruki huffed, turning away, her pink tongue peeking out—a protest against the youkai label.

Classes ended, and the classroom buzzed. June’s sky, nearing the solstice, stayed bright, perfect for outings.

“Hey, Kirishima, karaoke with us?”

“It’s a welcome party—our treat!”

“Wanna hear what you sing.”

“Uh, I—”

Surrounded by curious classmates, Hayato hesitated. Lacking peer experience and never having been to karaoke, he faltered.

A strong arm hooked his shoulder, pulling him.

“Let’s go!”

“Hey!”

“No way!” a sharp voice interrupted.

“Huh?”

“Nikaidou-san…?”

“…”

Even the group was surprised by Haruki. She, too, seemed startled by her outburst, coughing to recover.

“Uh, ahem. No can do. I was asked to show him around after school. Right, Kirishima-kun?”

“Oh, bummer. Lucky guy, having Nikaidou-san guide you!”

“W-Wait, Nikaidou-san…!?”

Grabbing his bag, Haruki dragged him with surprising strength, ignoring protests. It was less guiding, more hauling, straight out of school.

“Where are you taking me?”

“Just come on!”

They ran through a residential area, a beauty forcibly dragging him, embarrassing yet nostalgic—like childhood chases on rural paths, now on asphalt.

“Haha! Same old, huh!”

“Too slow!” Hayato teased, speeding up like old times.

“Mph!?” Haruki matched him, refusing to lose.

They traded leads, sprinting, hands linked, grinning fiercely. Laughter spilled.

“Haha!”

No logic, just joy in running together. Memories flooded back, confirming she was Haruki. Her appearance had changed, but something hadn’t—thrilling him.

“Here we are,” Haruki stopped.

“Huh, this is…”

An ordinary house in the neighborhood, unremarkable but unmistakable—Haruki’s home. Visiting each other was once routine.

“What’s up, Hayato?”

“Nothing.”

Her long hair, cool small hand, refined face—different, yet her carefree laugh irked him. Returning now felt like admitting defeat, so he muttered, “Thanks for having me.”

“No worries, just me here, so make yourself at home.”

“Seriously?”

Her casual reply, despite her polished look, threw him. The old roughneck’s vibe clashed with her beauty.

Suddenly, “Ah!” she exclaimed.

“Wait there! Just do it, okay!?”

“Hey!”

She dashed upstairs, skirt flipping, revealing plain boxer-style underwear. Clattering noises followed—likely frantic tidying. Guilt nagged Hayato.

Left alone at the entrance, he sighed. “Now what?”

“Sorry for the wait!”

“Wow.”

Her room, tidied (probably stuffed into a closet), had dark furniture, manga-heavy shelves, plastic models, and gaming consoles—a grown-up Haruki’s space. A small mirror and cosmetics on the desk were the only feminine touches; otherwise, it resembled Hayato’s room.

“Sit anywhere,” she tossed a cushion.

“Alright. Uh, Haruki—”

“Wanna take off your socks? It’s hot.”

“…Sure, but—”

She stripped hers off, revealing shapely feminine feet, catching him off guard. Sitting cross-legged on the cushion, leaning close, she evoked old Haruki, dispelling any allure. Hayato stifled a laugh.

Her glare turned accusatory. “So, who’s a monkey youkai?”

“Uh, well…”

She was clearly sulking over his morning comment, not truly mad. Her pout and creeping closer made him sweat.

“Sorry, my bad. A ‘debt,’ okay?”

“Hm, a ‘debt’? Fine.”

Satisfied, she dropped her frown, smirking at the word debt—a term special to them. A “debt” was a gift, never repaid or canceled, a unique rule between them.

“Debt, huh? Nostalgic. How many do you owe me, Hayato?”

“My line. You owe me plenty.”

“Haha, true.”

“Pfft.”

“Haha.”

Laughter spilled. Amid the mood, Hayato voiced a nagging thought.

“Your look is cheating, you know.”

“My face?”

He gestured at her refined, Yamato Nadeshiko appearance, far from the old roughneck—now marred by bare feet and cross-legged posture.

“Things happened, so I’m camouflaging like this.”

“Camouflage, huh? So you are a youk—”

“Hayato!”

“Haha, sorry. Another debt.”

“Ugh, you…”

Their old pattern—bickering, debts, making up—stacked memories, their version of apologies. Resuming this felt amusing yet embarrassing.

Avoiding her gaze, Hayato spotted something familiar.

“Still got that?”

“Games too, still loaded.”

“Nostalgic.”

“Let’s battle. Loser owes a debt.”

“Cheap debt.”

“Best of five.”

“Deal.”

It was an old kart-racing game with mushroom and turtle characters, a childhood obsession. Even now, they dove in.

“No way, cheating! How’d you get that item now!?”

“My good karma?”

“Liar! You called me a youkai!”

“Haha!”

No deep talk, just shoulder-to-shoulder gaming. Questions lingered, but game chatter sufficed. The distance between them shrank.

The early summer sun dipped, signaling time.

“Gotta go.”

“Oh… right.”

Fun passed quickly, leaving a pang of loneliness. They’d once thought their time together was eternal, until it abruptly ended.

Haruki pouted like a sulky child, watching him lace his shoes. Hayato felt her gaze and shared her sentiment.

To shake it off, he forced cheer. “See ya.”

“…”

It was their usual goodbye, laden with meaning. Haruki understood.

Her reply, their reunion greeting: “Yeah, see ya… and welcome back!”

“Welcome back?”

“It’s a welcome back for me.”

“Haha, what’s that?”

Her beaming smile, like a blooming flower, was the day’s brightest.



◇◇◇

Hayato’s new home, a ten-story family apartment near Haruki’s house, contrasted with Tsukino-se’s wooden single-story houses. Open rural doorways were now auto-locked entrances. Urban life was jarring, adaptation slow.

“I’m back.”

“Welcome home, Onii,” Himeko drawled.

“…Himeko, you’re showing everything.”

“Wanna see?”

“No, that’s why I said it.”

“Then don’t look.”

“Ugh.”

In their sixth-floor living room, his sister greeted lazily. Bright eyes, dyed wavy hair, a short but tasteful uniform skirt—she was a trendy girl, Hayato’s cute sister. But now, sprawled on the sofa, skirt flipped up, she looked sloppy, making him grimace.

(Sigh, Haruki, Himeko…)

Overlaying his friend and sister’s careless poses, he sighed. Only he saw them like this, he figured, resigned.

“Where’s Dad?”

“Hospital, visiting Mom.”

“Right. Dinner?”

“Your job. I’m busy.”

“Fine.”

Himeko fiddled with her phone, groaning occasionally. She’d sworn not to seem rural, likely researching to avoid slip-ups after her own transfer-student grilling.

“Just admit you’re a country girl.”

“Shut up, Onii!”

Her vanity caused past blunders. Checking the fridge, Hayato planned dinner.

(Pork block, leeks, peppers, cabbage, shiitake…)

After a meager lunch, he craved something hearty. He sliced pork, marinated it in soy sauce, sugar, mirin, starch, and sesame oil, then chopped vegetables roughly, using up leftovers. A mix of oyster sauce, doubanjiang, soy, and sake made the sauce. Stir-fried together, it became a makeshift qingtiao rousi, paired with rice and instant miso soup—decently colorful.

“Himeko, done.”

“Coming… Whoa.”

“What?”

“Still making bar snacks, huh?”

“It’s normal food, isn’t it?”

“Sure.”

Rural gatherings often involved banquets, where Hayato cooked snacks for pocket money, skewing his recipe repertoire.

“Itadakimasu.”

“Dig in.”

“Mm, goes great with rice. I’ll get fat! Oh, Onii, guess what?”

“Hm?”

“I Learned at school today—there’s no coin-operated rice polishers here.”

“What…?”

“And you can reach a station in ten minutes.”

“That’s a real station!”

The urban-rural gap shocked the Kirishima siblings. Himeko, too, faced transfer struggles.

“So, how’s it going?”

“What?”

“Something good happened, right?”

“Why?”

“You’re grinning.”

“…Huh?”

Himeko’s comment made Hayato notice his loosened cheeks. Reuniting with Haruki had him beaming.

“I met Haruki at school.”

“Haru-chan… No way, that Haru-chan!?”

“Get this—our seats are next to each other.”

“Wow! How’s she now?”

“Well…”

He pictured today’s Haruki. Once a muddy, scraped-up tomboy in shorts and a cap, now with glossy long hair, flawless skin, and a delicate Yamato Nadeshiko air. Yet her mischievous grin was unmistakably the same.

“She hasn’t changed. Still Haruki, she is racking up ‘debts’ already.”

“Cool! I wanna see her.”

“She’s stronger, more forceful—maybe evolved from monkey to gorilla.”

“Haha, what’s that?”

They bonded over their shared friend, memories resurfacing—uneven ice cream splits, cicada-catching contests, game battles. Countless “debts” marked their past.

That day, summer’s end, when their endless days crumbled. A small promise made then still lived.

Their heights, once equal, now differed by a head. Linked hands, once the same size, now varied. Strides, though paced alike, had grown apart.

Differences born in separation.

Yet, they felt surmountable.

A bond thought lost was restarting with the summer.
