Episode 9: How Could I Ignore You!
In the early summer afternoon, shouts from the gym class echoed across the school grounds, and the blazing sun shone through the classroom window.

During a drowsy classical literature class, Hayato dozed off. Suddenly, a folded note landed in front of him.

“Hm…?”

Only one person would do this. Sure enough, Haruki, seated next to him, grinned mischievously, her eyes darting to urge him to read it.

After school, my place! Hime-chan’s coming, help!

Hayato tilted his head, puzzled.

(What’s this?)

Going to Haruki’s was fine—he’d been there often. Himeko visiting made sense; they were childhood friends, and their late-night chat had been lively. But help!? What did that mean?

Glancing at Haruki, she only made a pleading gesture with one hand. Frustrated, Hayato scribbled a reply and tossed it back discreetly.

Help doesn’t explain anything. Did you fight with Himeko?

Haruki read it, wrote back, and tossed it. They traded notes secretly during the sleepy class.

Hime-chan found out I don’t own a single skirt in my casual wardrobe and chewed me out big time.

Sounds like you. Why’s that a problem?

She’s coming to my place to inspect my clothes, but do you think I can handle girly fashion talk?

…Nope. You’d probably get more excited about barbecue charcoal tricks.

Exactly—wait, what’s that? Now I want a barbecue! Ugh, we’d need a big rural space for that.

You stack the charcoal in a cylinder so air flows better.

Cool! Oh, are you still eating mostly boar or deer?

Depends on what gets caught in the traps for field pests, but mostly boar. Sometimes badgers—

The original topic derailed, but they got caught up in the random chatter, something they both enjoyed. However, their focus on secrecy slipped.

“Nikaidou, something up with Kirishima?” the teacher called.

“!”

“!?”

Snapping back, they flinched. Classmates were staring, some suspecting something between them, their curiosity undeniable.

Exchanging a glance, Haruki, looking genuinely apologetic, raised her hand.

“Um, sensei, Kirishima’s been fidgeting… I think he needs the bathroom…”

“What, Kirishima? If you need to go, say so. Hurry up, and do it before class next time.”

“What!?”

Laughter erupted. Some boys snickered, “How long’s he been holding it?” “Embarrassing dude.”

Nikaidou Haruki, the polished, popular honor student, was beyond doubt.

(She used me as an excuse!)

Blushing with embarrassment, Hayato glared at Haruki, who winked and stuck out her tongue.

“(Sorry, Hayato.)”

“(Tch, that’s a debt!)”

Unable to protest the bathroom narrative, Hayato, red-faced, shuffled out, looking like he’d been desperately holding it in.

The end-of-day chime rang, and the school buzzed with chatter as students discussed after-school plans.

“Sorry, I’ve got plans with a friend today,” Haruki said, a common excuse.

But coming from Nikaidou Haruki, it caused a stir.

“Nikaidou has plans with a friend… Who?”

“She doesn’t usually hang out with anyone…”

“Probably that childhood friend she mentioned this morning, right?”

Whispers spread.

(Being popular’s tough.)

Hayato caught the murmurs as he stood, intending to head home separately. A transfer student and a popular beauty shouldn’t have more than neighboring seats in common—a normal boundary.

At the school entrance, another commotion drew his attention.

“Who’s that girl? Her uniform’s from the nearby middle school, right?”

“She’s super cute! I’m in her school, but I’d remember someone like her.”

“A middle schooler here… Waiting for a boyfriend!?”

“What kinda guy gets a girl like that to pick him up? I gotta see his face!”

Curious, Hayato looked—and saw Himeko, his sister.

Overwhelmed by curious stares, unlike anything in rural Tsukino-se, she looked near tears, glancing around nervously.

(Himeko didn’t think about the attention…)

Sighing, Hayato pressed his temple. To him, she was just his anxious sister, but to others, her nervous anticipation for someone seemed endearing, earning mostly positive vibes.

Spotting her target, the timid “small animal” sprinted like a puppy wagging its tail.

“Haru-chan!”

“Hime-chan!?”

Himeko grabbed Haruki’s arm, urging her to hurry, desperate to escape the crowd. To onlookers, she was boldly tugging the arm of the famous Nikaidou Haruki.

Dressed up for the high school visit, Himeko’s styled hair and makeup matched Haruki’s charm, sparking comments like, “Who’s she? She’s gorgeous!” “Someone said she’s a childhood friend reunited.” The attention made her even more uncomfortable.

“…”

Hayato watched them go amid the whispers, then headed to Haruki’s house a bit later, his chest inexplicably restless, his face tinged with unease. Early summer clouds veiled the still-high sun.

Hayato walked alone to Haruki’s house, where Himeko had dragged her.

“So hot…”

Heat shimmered off the asphalt, the occasional breeze lukewarm and stifling.

Was his moody haze due to the oppressive weather or childish jealousy? He couldn’t tell.

His steps slowed, as if delaying an answer, but he eventually reached Haruki’s ordinary residential house—a place he’d visited often.

About to ring the intercom, he recalled Haruki vanishing into its darkness the other night. Hesitating, his hand froze.

“Ugh, damn it’s hot!”

Scratching his head to brush off the feeling, he jabbed the intercom.

The chime rang, followed by a brief silence, then frantic footsteps as the door flew open.

“Here, Hayato’s here! See!?” Haruki shouted.

“Onii, grab Haru-chan!” Himeko urged.

“What!?”

Haruki bolted out, and Hayato, as instructed, caught her in a near-hug.

“Traitor, Hayato!”

“No clue what’s happening, but calm down, Haruki.”

Hayato was bewildered. He didn’t understand their situation, and Haruki’s smaller frame—unlike childhood—fit snugly in his arms. Her soft arms, warmth, and the accidental brushes of her chest and thighs flustered him further.

“Hehe, Onii, bring her in.”

“Uh, okay…”

“Kill me now!”

Himeko’s odd intensity suggested compliance was wise. Haruki, slumping, was dragged back, muttering “kukkoru” like a playful act, hinting at some leeway.

“Ugh…”

Stepping into Haruki’s now-familiar room, Hayato recoiled. Fashion magazines and Himeko’s cosmetics covered the desk, screaming “girls only.” No wonder Haruki tried to flee.

On the bed, drab, tasteless shirts, shorts, and pants were displayed like a warning. Hayato glanced at Haruki.

“Onii, what do you think of this fashionless, unsexy wardrobe?”

“Even I can tell it’s rough.”

“Hayato!?”

Haruki glared, betrayed, but Hayato’s pitying look made her falter.

Himeko and Hayato inspected her disastrous clothing.

“Haru-chan, these are all dark, frumpy, and worn out. Some don’t even fit, like this shirt.”

“They’re like ‘I don’t care if they get dirty’ clothes, straight out of childhood.”

“That one time was a miracle…”

“Yeah, a miracle…”

“Ugh…”

Critiqued by her childhood friends, Haruki teared up, slumping with a faint, “Are they that bad?”

Himeko’s gaze softened.

“It’s okay, Haru-chan. Even if your awful childhood sense makes me not want to walk with you, you’re still you. I won’t abandon you.”

“I’m that bad!?”

“Himeko?”

Himeko’s bright smile turned sly as she whispered to Haruki. Initially startled, Haruki grew serious, then flashed the mischievous grin she often aimed at Hayato.

(What’s she planting? Looks like brainwashing.)

Hayato, exasperated, watched the girls scheme, their occasional glances at him reminiscent of childhood antics.

“So, interested?”

“Yes, sensei!”

“Good, then—”

Haruki saluted, and Himeko nodded, launching a fashion lecture using magazines as textbooks.

Hayato barely followed, and Haruki seemed half-engaged, but Himeko pressed on relentlessly.

(This is like their childhood pretend play.)

Himeko’s bossy moments used to drag them along, and this felt the same. Hayato stifled a chuckle.

Himeko dominated, Hayato nodded mechanically to her prompts, and Haruki’s eyes spun, practically steaming. Yet, their shared glances were all smiles.

“That’s it. Ugh, talking so much made me thirsty.”

“Oh, I haven’t offered anything. Tea okay?”

Haruki stood, heading downstairs to the kitchen.

Hayato sighed, stretching. Himeko shot him a reproachful look.

“Onii, Haru-chan’s still Haru-chan, but she’s a girl too, you know?”

“That’s harsh.”

“Go help her.”

“Ugh.”

Grumbling, Hayato stood, partly because Himeko’s words recalled Haruki’s soft touch, a feeling he didn’t want his sister to see.

Complex emotions swirled as he scratched his head, descending the stairs.

He’d never been beyond Haruki’s room but found the kitchen easily, its light spilling through a cracked door.

“Haruki, I’ll help carry stuff.”

“…Oh.”

Her voice sounded dazed. Hayato’s eyes caught the scene beyond.

The kitchen opened to a typical LDK layout, but the living room felt off. Scattered flyers, paper bags, closed shutters despite daylight, dusty yet overly tidy furniture—no signs of recent use.

Shifting to the kitchen, he saw trash bags stuffed with bento containers and frozen food packaging, vividly revealing Haruki’s reality.

“…Haruki.”

“Ah, haha…”

A strained word escaped Hayato. Haruki only offered a troubled smile.

Everything clicked: her “disguise,” convenience store bentos, frozen food hauls, the dark house, this lifeless living room, her only-child status. Her room was filled with solo hobbies—games, manga, models.

Hayato realized he’d never met anyone else here, nor felt their presence. Haruki had likely been alone in this house for years, yet she smiled, pretending everything was fine.

That,

Infuriated him.

More than that, he was furious at himself for not noticing sooner.

“…Let’s go, Haruki.”

“W-Wait, Hayato! Go where…?”

Shutting off the stove, he grabbed her arm. To her, he looked angry, and her protest faded.

“Onii, Haru-chan, what’s wrong?”

Himeko, alarmed by Haruki’s loud voice, came to check. She saw Hayato dragging her childhood friend somewhere.

About to speak, she stopped, reading his stern, unyielding expression. From experience, she knew this face meant he was acting for someone’s sake—and he was dependable.

“Himeko, we’re hitting the supermarket, then home.”

“Got it. I’ll pack.”

“Hime-chan too!?”

Confused, Haruki was pulled along by the resolute siblings.

She didn’t understand why, but knowing it was for her sake, she didn’t feel bad.

“Um, can I change first…?”

“Uniform’s fine, right?”

“Y-Yes.”

Haruki’s request to change was shot down by Himeko’s dazzling smile.

At a ten-story family apartment, six floors up, the Kirishima kitchen was lined with supermarket groceries.

“Uh…”

Haruki, dragged here by Hayato and Himeko, was lost.

“I’ll chop veggies. Himeko, grate garlic and ginger.”

“Roger. Haru-chan, grab a bowl and seasonings.”

“Uh, sure… Where are they?”

Haruki understood they were cooking dinner but not why or what. She followed their instructions, assisting.

(Hayato’s so skilled…)

Seeing him cook for the first time, his deft movements impressed her, hinting at deep experience. She found it curious.

Cooking progressed steadily. Ground pork, chopped chives, cabbage, napa, leeks, shiitake, grated garlic, ginger, salt, pepper, and soy sauce were mixed into a filling, kneaded thoroughly.

When round dumpling wrappers appeared, Haruki guessed.

“Gyoza? That’s a lot, though.”

“It’s a hassle to make, so with extra hands, we’ll make a ton and freeze the rest.”

“Haha, making your guest work?”

“It’s you, Haruki.”

“Because it’s me… Heh, got it.”

They wrapped gyoza in silence. Haruki struggled at first but, true to her honor-student nature, quickly mastered it, impressing Hayato. Himeko, meanwhile, produced misshapen gyoza sculptures.

“Hime-chan… Wow…”

“Shut it! I’m fine with Onii cooking!”

“No harm in learning…”

Himeko kept wrapping, undeterred. Their smiles showed they enjoyed the process.

The gyoza were pan-fried with oil, steamed with flour-water, yielding perfect winged dumplings. Paired with rice, miso soup, and Minamo Mitake’s pickled eggplant, dinner was ready.

“Wow…!”

Haruki gasped at the spread, a surge of excitement welling up.

“Let’s eat—hot! Onii, water!”

“What’re you doing, Himeko… Not eating, Haruki?”

“Oh, right. Itadakimasu.”

Himeko yelped at the heat, Hayato fetched water, and Haruki bit into a gyoza.

“Hot, but delicious.”

It was delectable, nostalgic, extraordinary. Sharing a meal like this was rare for her.

Chopsticks flew, the heaping gyoza plate dwindled, and Haruki and Himeko playfully fought over the last ones.

“Eat rice too, rice!”

“But Haru-chan’s hogging them!”

“Mmph, mmm!”

Their silly, childish banter filled the meal. Haruki felt her heart, not just her stomach, being nourished.

(I was more fragile than I thought…)

That’s why Hayato dragged her here, she realized. But he, unsure what to say, chose this shared meal. Smirking at his clumsiness, she noticed tears welling, quickly wiping them.

Hayato, seeing her, scratched his head awkwardly before speaking.

“Uh… Usually, it’s just me and Himeko for dinner. So, if you’re okay with it, wanna eat with us from now on?”

“…Huh?”

Haruki blanked, not processing. Questions about their family or his cooking skill flashed through her mind, but she voiced a simpler one.

“Why?”

She couldn’t fathom why he’d go this far. His kindness confused her, used to intentions laced with ulterior motives.

She had her own unspoken burdens, things Hayato must be curious about. Yet he said nothing, just offered a hand. Why?

“Because it’s you… Because we’re friends.”

“…Oh.”

His simple reason hit hard, her tears nearly spilling. She wiped them hastily.

“So, because it’s me, huh?”

“Yeah.”

“Another debt?”

“No need for that.”

“Then… a promise.”

“…Here.”

Gruffly, Hayato linked pinkies with Haruki’s mischievous grin.

They had things left unsaid, but some things didn’t need words.

“Geez, you two are as close as ever…” Himeko said, half-exasperated, half-envious.
