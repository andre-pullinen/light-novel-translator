Episode 13: Didn’t We Make a Promise?
On a Sunday morning, Hayato and Haruki plan to go buy a smartphone. At the Kirishima household, Hayato’s younger sister Himeko, who has been hogging the TV since early morning, looks puzzled when she sees her brother preparing to head to the city.

“Onii, a straw hat? Seriously?”
“Huh? Isn’t it good for heatstroke?”
“There’s plenty of shade from buildings, and tons of cool shops to rest in. It’s not like Tsukino-se with nothing around.”
“Guess you’re right…”

Hayato wears a plain shirt and denim pants—safe choices. But the straw hat makes him look like he’s heading to a farm, prompting Himeko to tease him. She shakes her head at her clueless brother.

“Why meet up there? Your houses are close; just go together.”
“Huh!?”

Himeko’s words make her realize Hayato’s obliviousness. She pauses her recording to face him.

“Listen, Onii. Yesterday, I went shopping with Haru-chan for clothes.”
“Yeah, you did.”
“That means it’s the first time you’ll see her like that. You need a proper stage for her reveal, not just outside the house. Get it?”
“Hmm, is that how it works?”
“Yes, that’s how it works.”

Seeing Hayato’s confusion, Himeko sighs.

(Telling Onii to understand a girl’s heart is asking too much… and Haru-chan’s no better.)

She sighs again, exasperated, and turns back to the TV.

“Oh, Onii, don’t forget the papers on the table.”
“This… parental consent and Dad’s account?”
“Yup, I downloaded and stamped them. Same carrier as me, right?”
“Carrier?”
“Phone company. You don’t even know that?”
“Sorry, thanks for the trouble—!?”
“What!?”

Suddenly, Hayato lets out a weird sound, his eyes glued to the TV. Himeko makes a similar noise. The drama is at a climax: a cheating couple in bed, interrupted by the wife storming in with a knife. It’s an intense scene, making Hayato’s reaction understandable. Even Himeko blushes and freezes awkwardly.

“Uh, is this… popular now?”
“It’s Ten Years of Solitude! A classmate said it’s good! The actress is amazing, even if her personal life’s messy—divorced twice, loves drama—but her acting’s so real…”
“Himeko.”
“…Yes, I’ll study for exams too.”

Himeko wilts under Hayato’s stern gaze.

The meeting spot is the station near the shop. It’s a 20-minute train ride from their local station, the largest in the area.

“Where’s the east exit…?”

The station is a maze of private railways, overground and underground. Hayato, already shocked by a hospital visit yesterday, feels overwhelmed.

“Damn, I’m late! Haruki’s gonna hold this over me!”

Despite leaving early, he’s lost in the station, wandering like a child. Frustration grows—he wants to reach Haruki but feels stuck. He even thinks it’d be fun to navigate this with her, making his delay feel wasteful. He just wants to see her.

By the time he reaches the bird statue meeting point, he’s five minutes late. The Sunday crowd makes spotting Haruki tough. Realizing he needs a phone, he raises a hand to shout—

“You’re here!”
“…Huh?”

A girl suddenly grabs his arm and pulls him away. Two sleazy guys chase after her, clearly trying to hit on her.

“She’s really with a guy?”
“Forget that late loser, come with us!”

The girl is strikingly beautiful: a white summer dress with frilled hems and shoulder straps, long black hair in a half-up braid, looking like a refined young lady. Hayato’s never seen anyone like her. No wonder guys approach her while she waits alone.

“Let’s go!”
“Whoa, wait!”

She drags him outside, desperate to escape. Her grip is strong, her strides wide. Hayato’s too stunned to react, swept along. As a teenage boy, being clung to by such a beauty—feeling her softness through thin fabric—throws him into a panic. Her sweet scent and close contact make his heart race.

Yet, her pulling feels familiar. Looking at her, her features overlap with someone he knows. Her annoyed glance confirms it.

“Haruki…?”
“…!”

Outside the station, the girl—Haruki—stops and turns, fuming.

“Ugh, Hayato, you’re late! I was waiting to surprise you, but I got surprised by those creeps! Worst day ever!”
“Sorry… the station was too big, I got lost.”
“I told you, a smartphone’s essential!”
“…I get it now.”



Haruki pouts, but something’s different. Her face is the same, yet her dress and subtle makeup make her captivating. Hayato’s heart skips, unable to look directly at her. She notices his red face and smirks mischievously.

“Oho, is Hayato blushing?”
“No way! Are you… wearing makeup?”
“Yup, first time trying. How’s it look?”

She twirls, her long hair flowing, the dress’s short skirt flaring, revealing toned thighs. Hayato’s pulse spikes. Feeling teased, he scratches his head to hide his fluster, but Haruki’s already won. She reaches for his arm to tease more—

“You look… really cute. It suits you.”
“M-Mya!?”

Haruki’s face turns redder than Hayato’s, steam practically rising. His honest words hit hard because they’re from him. Overwhelmed, she stammers.

“T-That’s, um, it’s, uh, Hayato!”
“Yeah, you wore it for me, right?”
“N-No… well, yes, but—Mya! Aah!”
“Haruki!?”
“Let’s go pick a phone already!”

Flustered, Haruki grabs his hand and marches off. Both red-faced, they calm down but avoid revisiting the topic. Haruki whispers softly.

“…Thanks.”
“…Sure.”

The sky is clear, promising a hot day.

The carrier shop is near the station, used by both Himeko and Haruki. Hayato’s nervous in the unfamiliar store, overwhelmed by new terms and plans.

“Uh, Haruki?”
“Alright, this means…”

Haruki, ever the top student, explains the clerk’s words clearly, despite Hayato’s panic. An hour later, he has a phone.

“Phew, thanks, Haruki. I’d be lost without you.”
“No problem. Sure you wanted the same as mine? There were other options.”
“Matching’s better, right?”
“M-Matching!? Hayato!?”
“If I have questions, I can ask you.”
“Oh, right. But Himeko’s phone would work too.”
“She’s my sister. It’s… awkward.”
“Hmm, I don’t get it.”

Their goal achieved, they wander the city. Neither wants to leave yet. For Hayato, the urban jungle—concrete, vending machines, countless crosswalks—is thrilling. He gawks like a tourist.

Noticing Haruki’s awkward gait, he glances at her new heeled sandals.

“Sorry, walking too fast? Those shoes new?”
“What? No, I’m fine.”
“You looked uncomfortable.”
“Uh…”

Haruki hesitates, then whispers embarrassedly.

“The skirt’s… short.”
“Huh?”

Her dress is much shorter than her usual knee-length uniform, exposing her thighs. It’s not too short, but she’s self-conscious.

“It’s thin, breezy, and could flip up. Bending over’s risky too.”
“That sounds… tough.”
“I realized mini-skirts are like discipline tools. Girls wearing them are basically training.”
“Really?”
“Yup. It’s unfair I’m the only one struggling. You try it!”
“No way, it’d look awful on me.”
“Hmm, you’re Himeko’s brother, it’d suit you. I get why Himeko was so excited yesterday!”
“Stop it, your eyes are serious!”

They banter, slowing their pace. Hayato scans for a shop, overwhelmed by the city’s signs. Haruki suggests using his phone.

“Search the store and city name—”
“Whoa, it worked! Amazing!”

Haruki smiles at his excitement.

Ten minutes from the station, they find a massive five-story, 100-yen store.

“It’s huge…”
“Wow, bigger than I heard!”
“You’ve never been here?”
“Nope, I’m usually a homebody.”
“Let’s go!”
“Wait, Hayato!?”

Hayato pulls her inside, ignoring her shy hesitation. The sheer variety—food, dishes, cosmetics, toys, tools—stuns them.

“No way, all this for 100 yen!?”
“Look, food’s pricier than supermarket sales.”
“Oh, right.”

They’re drawn to different sections, their desire to buy growing. Hayato eyes dishes, Haruki a moss ball.

“Haruki, do you need that moss?”
“I know I don’t, but it’s calling me!”
“Calm down, what’s it for? A terrarium?”
“I just want it!”
“Snap out of it!”

They roam like kids in the countryside, exploring without purpose, just enjoying each other’s company. Haruki’s gender doesn’t change their bond—she’s his irreplaceable friend.

“…We didn’t buy anything.”
“Haha, you’ve always been indecisive. Remember choosing candy at Grandma Murao’s?”
“We’d settle on ramune and ice cream.”
“Yeah, nostalgic…”

Haruki’s face turns wistful, then sad. Hayato’s chest tightens.

“Haru—”

Gurgle.

“Haha, hungry?”
“Yup, it’s past one. Eat somewhere?”
“I know a place!”

Haruki’s usual playful grin returns, but Hayato senses something off.

“What the heck!?”
“Pretty cool, right?”

Karaoke Celery isn’t what Hayato expects. The tropical-themed lobby and cushioned, shoe-off room feel like a party space, not the banquet hall karaoke of Tsukino-se.

“I’m hungry and tired.”
“Hey!”

Haruki kicks off her sandals, dives onto the cushions, and fiddles with a tablet, relaxed like she’s home. Her short dress reveals boxer shorts, carefree. Despite her beauty, Hayato sighs, fixing her skirt.

“Oops, got too comfy after stressing about exposure. Perk for you?”
“It’s just like seeing Himeko.”
“Grr, you know I’m pretty popular, right?”

Haruki, competitive, undoes her dress’s shoulder strap, posing seductively.

“How’s this, Hayato?”
“Haru…ki!”

His throat catches. Her confident charm shatters his “friend” filter, but her playful eyes frustrate him. A staff member interrupts.

“Here’s your banana maple syrup pudding honey toast!”
“Eek!”

They jump apart, sitting formally, faces red. The young staff serves, but warns:

“This isn’t that kind of place, so please refrain.”



“Mya!?”

The door shuts. The staff noticed Haruki’s undone strap. To outsiders, they look like a couple, no excuses.

“I’m not shameless!”
“Calm down, but you kinda are.”
“Mya! Aah!”

Mortified, Haruki attacks the massive honey toast—a loaf topped with banana, pudding, ice cream, and cream. Hayato joins her, both stress-eating.

They catch each other’s silly faces and laugh.

“What are we doing?”
“Such idiots.”

They laugh like kids again.

An hour later, too awkward to sing, they leave. Haruki stretches, the air reset, reminding Hayato of their childhood fights that vanished by morning.

“We didn’t sing at karaoke.”
“Just ate lunch.”
“Next time, then.”
“…Yeah, next time!”

Haruki’s eyes light up, but she stares at Hayato, tilting her head.

“Hayato, you’re a boy, right?”
“Huh? What’s that about?”
“Earlier, we… but you’re you, and I’m me. What are we?”
“…That’s tough.”
“Yeah, tough.”

Their relationship is complex. They’re different now—height, hands, habits—but their shared past anchors them. They’re still Hayato and Haruki.

“Still, we’re us, right?”
“Yeah, us.”

They laugh, and Hayato instinctively takes her hand. Realizing it, he tries to let go, but Haruki grips tighter, blushing but nodding.

“You always drag me around.”
“Do I?”
“Really, Hayato.”

She pulls him toward a crowd, their dynamic unchanged. But Hayato feels they’re reclaiming something lost.

“…!”
“Haruki?”

In the crowd, Haruki freezes, her face pale. A film crew shoots a drama—Ten Years of Solitude. A stunning actress, Mao Takura, captivates the onlookers, her beauty defying her age. Whispers mix awe with gossip.

Hayato recognizes her from Himeko’s drama, feeling déjà vu. But Haruki’s reaction is extreme—pale, trembling, nails digging into his hand. Her face is a storm of emotions.

“Haruki!”

She lets go and runs, head down, heels clacking. Hayato chases, seeing her fight tears. She’s stubborn, hiding pain. He doesn’t know why but can’t leave her alone.

Her solitary life, the empty house—clues swirl in his mind, but something’s missing.

(I can’t leave her alone!)

He chases, desperate not to lose her. She’s close, yet distant.

“…!”
“Got you!”

Haruki trips, and he catches her. She looks away, silent.

“Can we walk along the tracks to get home?”
“Dunno.”
“Let’s try.”
“Hayato?”

They walk the urban road, hand in hand, the air heavy. Haruki’s face hides her emotions. Hayato feels nostalgia—she’s like the lonely Haruki he first met, stubborn and waiting. He pulled her to play then, and now he pulls her again, unchanged.

“You’re always so forceful.”
“Am I?”
“Your hand’s big and rough.”
“From farm work.”
“I used to be taller.”
“Was I?”
“Yup.”
“Don’t remember.”
“I do.”

Haruki grips his hand, smiling faintly, fragile. Hayato grips back, wordless but present. They walk side by side, not quite like before. Their height, hands, her dress—seven years changed them. Yet, their clasped hands hold something constant.

“Hayato, can you hear one complaint?”
“Sure.”
“I live alone in that house.”

Her voice pretends normalcy. A parent and child pass, holding hands. Haruki stops, her voice trembling.

“I’m being good, waiting.”

Her words, full of pain, are drowned by traffic. The sun casts building shadows.

“Just kidding.”

She forces a bright tone, loosening her grip.

“…Right.”

They banter, returning to normal, spotting odd shops. But it’s Haruki’s deflection, a wall from seven years apart. Her smile stings Hayato.

They reach their station.

“Our stop already.”
“Yup.”
“Too early for dinner. I’ll change—this skirt’s tricky.”
“No!”
“Hayato? I have normal clothes—”
“No.”
“Uh…”
“No.”

Hayato drags her, stubborn.

“I… haven’t had enough fun with you.”
“Oh… okay.”

He can’t let her return to that house. He pulls her to his home, unaware of his bold words.

At 4 p.m., they arrive. Himeko, caught playing a fitness game, holds a controller, study materials nearby.

“Muscles are lifelong buddies!”
“Knowledge too.”
“Ugh, I needed a break!”

Himeko’s excuses falter. Haruki laughs at the sibling bickering, easing the tension.

“Himeko’s got exams, right? Breaks are important. Let’s play that game together!”
“Yeah! Onii?”
“…Fine.”

They play, taking turns, cheering and teasing. Haruki’s energetic moves in her dress risk exposure.

“Haru-chan’s flashing!”
“Stop sounding like an old man.”
“So, Onii, how’s Haru-chan’s look?”
“Cute, I guess… but it’s Haruki.”

Hayato sees her as a sister, averting his eyes. Haruki boasts her high score, oblivious to her skirt.

Dinner is katsu curry, a hit with everyone. Himeko tries to game again, but Haruki tutors her.

“Substitute 4 for y—”
“Where’d 4 come from!?”
“From the first equation.”
“You didn’t solve it yet!?”

Haruki’s teaching is chaotic but fun. Hayato washes dishes, reflecting on the day.

(I can’t ignore it.)

Haruki’s pained face lingers.

“It’s getting late. I should go.”
“Past 9 already. Onii, walk her.”
“Yeah…”

Something nags at him. At the door, they find a downpour.

“Wow, pouring!”
“Total deluge.”
“Can I borrow an umbrella?”
“Sure…”

The mood is somber. Haruki prepares to run.

“This rain’s too much. I’ll run. Here’s your umbrella.”
“Wait!”

Hayato grabs her arm, serious.

“Don’t go. Stay over.”
“…What?”

Her shock is swallowed by the rain’s roar.

The rain pounds outside, but the apartment’s dryer hums louder. In the bath, Hayato groans.

“What am I doing…?”

Recalling his bold invitation, he blushes.

“Stay over!? That’s bold! Am I getting… you know!?”
“No! The rain, your dress, you’ll catch a cold!”
“So you want to stay with me that bad!?”
“…Yeah, damn it! Stay!”
“Seriously!? We’re not like that, it’s too soon, Himeko’s here!”
“Himeko’ll be thrilled. I just—”

He insisted she not return home, haunted by her lonely expression. Himeko’s excitement at a “sleepover” saved the moment.

He exits the bath, hair damp, pondering sleeping arrangements.

“Welcome back! Borrowing stuff.”

Haruki’s on his bed, reading manga, wearing his oversized T-shirt, hair in pigtails. She’s relaxed, legs kicking.

“No dirty mags? In your PC?”
“Idiot. That’s my shirt? Not Himeko’s?”
“Uh…”

She sits up, serious.

“Himeko and I are almost the same size, but her shirts were tight… on my chest. She threw your shirt at me.”
“…Pfft! Himeko’s sensitive about that.”
“What do I do!? She’s super sulky! You’re her brother, help!”
“Ow, stop hitting! Give her sweets!”
“That’s lazy!”

Haruki’s upset, but Hayato laughs, knowing Himeko’s insecurity.

“Himeko’s growing up, caring about that stuff…”
“Haruki?”

She grips his shirt, feeling his back.

“You’re so big now. We used to be similar, but you’ve grown. Himeko’s taller than me, you’re great at cooking… we’re not kids anymore.”
“Yeah, a lot’s happened.”
“Right…”

Her voice is heavy, still affected. Hayato’s glad she’s here but lacks words to dig deeper. Seven years created a gap.

“Today was fun.”
“Huh?”
“Despite everything, hanging out with you was great.”
“…!”

His face burns, but he needs to say it.

“When you left, I was lonely. No friends my age. Since you came back, every day’s fun. I’m so glad we reunited.”
“Hayato…”
“I don’t know what’s going on with you. I can’t do much, but I can be there. With a phone now, I can come running—”

He looks up to see Haruki crying, tears streaming. She wipes them, confused.

“Why am I…?”

Her emotions overflow. Hayato turns away, giving her space.

“Here, borrow this.”
“…I’ll take it.”

She presses her forehead to his back, sobbing. Her tears, held for seven years, pour out. Hayato clenches his fists, angry at his helplessness.

After a while, her grip loosens.

“Haruki?”

She’s asleep, exhausted. Her peaceful face shows trust, making Hayato’s heart ache.



He carries her to bed, shocked at her lightness.

(So small, carrying so much…)

He lays her down gently, covering her. Her serene face stirs mixed emotions.

“…Mom…”

Her sleep-talk jolts him. He wipes her tears and stands.

“Goodnight.”

He turns off the light.

“Onii.”
“Himeko.”

Himeko waits outside, concerned. They share a wry smile, relieved. As Hayato heads to the living room, Himeko tugs his shirt.

“Your back’s a mess. Change.”
“Ugh, snot too?”
“Haru-chan’s something else.”
“Yeah.”

They laugh softly, their bond unchanged.
