Episode 3: An Ordinary Dinner Table
The Kirishima family had just moved in.

Dishes, summer clothes, stationery, notebooks, and textbooks—bare essentials were unpacked, but many boxes remained. Hayato’s room held several cardboard stacks.

“Let’s do this,” he muttered.

After returning home, Hayato dove into unpacking, barely changing. For days, he’d be swamped with organizing. Unlike the spacious rural house in Tsukino-se, the 3LDK apartment’s limited space challenged him.

“Onii, you here? Coming in,” Himeko called.

“Hm?”

Without waiting, Himeko entered, still in her uniform, phone in hand.

“Message from Dad. He’s at Mom’s again tonight.”

“No dinner needed, then.”

“Yup. Seriously, Onii, get a phone. I’m your messenger now.”

“Haha, my bad.”

“Ugh…!” Himeko huffed.

Ignoring her exasperation, Hayato checked the fridge. After yesterday’s and today’s cooking spree, it was nearly empty.

“Nothing… Anything in the freezer?”

“Hey, Onii,” Himeko tugged his shirt.

“Hm?”

Turning, he saw her face—strained, melancholic, reminiscent of that time. But she forced a cheery tone.

“I’m craving your greedy hamburger today.”

“That’s a lot of work. We’d need to shop too.”

“I know. I’ll help, okay?”

“Tch.”

Faced with her pleading look, Hayato couldn’t refuse. Himeko, clutching the household wallet, hurried him along, clearly intent on joining. She didn’t want to be alone.

He ruffled her hair, a bit roughly.

“Hey! What’s that for? You’ll mess up my hair!”



“It’s fine. That’s why we transferred her, right?”

“…Yeah,” she mumbled.

“Let’s go before it gets dark. I’m still fuzzy on the supermarket’s location.”

“Ugh, you’re hopeless, Onii.”

Hayato kept his voice light. Leaving, Himeko walked faster, as if shaking off unease, pulling him along.

The nearby supermarket was utterly ordinary.

Vegetables, meat, fish, dairy, drinks, seasonings, colorful snacks, and small goods—nothing remarkable. But to the Kirishima siblings, it was different.

In Tsukino-se, shopping meant a quirky general store, the co-op, or a distant roadside station. To Hayato and Himeko, this supermarket was a food amusement park.

“Deli section… Croquettes that take so much effort, only 38 yen each!?”

“Onii, look! Frozen waffles and cakes!”

“So many pasta types, I can’t tell them apart…”

“Dressing varieties are overwhelming…!”

They struggled to avoid splurging. That day, the Kirishima rule of a 200-yen snack limit was set.

Back home, night had fallen.

Lighting the kitchen, Hayato started cooking.

“Himeko, chop the vegetables. I’ll handle the mushrooms.”

“Got it. Vegetables are so expensive here… This really is a greedy hamburger.”

“Haha, in Tsukino-se, we got more mushrooms and veggies than we could eat.”

The greedy hamburger was a veggie-packed patty, bulked up with cabbage and eggplant alongside onions, their sweetness and the meat’s fat enhancing the flavor. Once, they’d thrown in any gifted produce, but trial and error settled on these staples.

The sauce was a Japanese-style mushroom mix—shiitake, shimeji, maitake, enoki, and eryngii—simmered in soy sauce, mirin, and sugar, thickened with cornstarch. Versatile, it suited fish, omelet rice, or pasta, so extras went into a container for the fridge.

Vegetable scraps went into miso soup, completing dinner.

“Itadakimasu… Ow, hot! Onii, water!”

“Seriously, what are you doing…?”

By the time dinner was ready, it was well past 8 p.m.

Hayato sighed, fetching water for Himeko, who yelped at the heat. Her eager eating softened his expression. The bold-flavored hamburger, paired with hunger, made rice vanish quickly.

“Your sauce tastes like it belongs with booze,” Himeko said.

“You’ve never drunk, have you? Got the shochu seal of approval, though.”

“Hehe, it’s delicious.”

“Glad.”

“…Wasn’t the greedy hamburger the first thing you made? It’s my memory dish… You’ve gotten really good at cooking, Onii.”

“…Yeah.”

“So, keep it up, okay?”

“Learn some yourself.”

“I’m a pro at frozen and instant!”

“Tch.”

Hayato and Himeko, brother and sister, at a lively yet slightly lonely table for two—too large for just them, but an utterly ordinary scene.
