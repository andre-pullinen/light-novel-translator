Episode 5: A Reliable Back
During a lunch break, the freedom from tedious classes brings varied actions at Himeko’s middle school: some rush to the cafeteria, others gather with friends to eat bento, or head to other classrooms or clubrooms. Himeko loves this time.

Bento, especially, feels grown-up compared to Tsukinose’s shared elementary-middle school lunches. It’s a simple joy for her.

“Ugh…”

Opening her bento, Himeko grimaces. Her friend Honoka Torikai peeks, seeing an ordinary bento: omurice, mini tomatoes, and broccoli for color.

“What’s wrong, Himeko-chan? Looks tasty,” Honoka says.

“…I hate tomatoes,” Himeko replies.

“Oh, just something you don’t like?”

“Not just that. It’s onii’s revenge. I kept teasing his dinners for looking awful, even yesterday.”

“Wait, your brother made this!?” Honoka exclaims.

“Yup, he always does, but…” Himeko trails off, then offers, “Wanna try a bite?”

“Absolutely!”

Overwhelmed, Himeko scoops a portion onto Honoka’s bento lid. Honoka inspects it, murmuring “Ooh” and “Wow,” making Himeko, though not the cook, feel shy about her brother’s work.

“Here goes… Oh! Tofu—no, okara!? Tons of mushrooms, more Japanese-style than chicken rice… So good! Your brother’s not just good—he’s amazing at cooking!” Honoka raves.

“He mostly makes bar snacks. Plus, he’s rough waking me up, cleans my room without asking—not that great,” Himeko grumbles.

“What… Not only cooks and makes bento, but wakes you up, cleans your room, and looks after you…?” Honoka gasps.

“Honoka-chan!?” Himeko squeaks.

Himeko meant to complain, but Honoka’s eyes widen, leaning forward to grab her hands. Others join in.

“Kirishima-san, tell us more about your brother!” one demands.

“What’s he like? Tall? Where’s his school? Got pics? Most importantly, does he have a girlfriend!?” another presses.

“A good-looking brother who cooks and cares… High points!”

“Introduce us this weekend!?”

“Uh, um…?” Himeko stammers.

To Himeko, Hayato’s a nag, meddlesome, and lacks tact. She’s grateful for meals, but his messy hair and straw-hat-in-the-city fashion scream no girlfriend material. She doesn’t get her friends’ interest.

But to them, Himeko’s a beauty despite quirks, so expectations for her brother are high.

“He’s not that great! Acts superior despite being one year older, dresses tacky, never had a girlfriend, and never will!” Himeko protests.

“Don’t say that, Himeko-chan!”

“Bring him to hang out!”

“Karaoke’s fine!”

“Onii’s… Wait, karaoke!? Not like singing at community halls or buses, but actual karaoke boxes!?” Himeko exclaims.

“…” The girls pause.

Now Himeko leans forward. Tsukinose had no karaoke shops, just outdated machines in community centers with old songs. Karaoke with friends after school is a dream from stories, sparkling in her eyes.

Her friends’ gazes soften.

“Let’s hit karaoke after school,” one suggests.

“Station one? I’ve got coupons.”

“Big sisters’ll cover Himeko-chan’s share!”

“W-What!?” Himeko blinks.

Her confusion aside, the girls’ excitement sets the afternoon’s plan.

Summer evenings linger. After an hour and a half of singing with four girls, the sun’s still up as Himeko trudges alone through the station’s shopping street.

“…Haa,” she sighs, recalling karaoke.

(They were so good…)

Her friends, especially Honoka, sang skillfully and kept the energy high. Himeko, a karaoke newbie, fumbled the touch panel, caused mic feedback, and mumbled more than sang. Honoka’s group watched with warm—Himeko felt teasing—smiles, leaving her a bit down.

(Next time, I’ll practice with onii or Haru-chan!)

Quick to rebound, Himeko resolves, then spots a familiar face.

“Hime-chan!?”

“Haru-chan!?” Himeko starts, but girls in Haruki’s uniform surround her.

Shy Himeko freezes as they chatter.

“This girl! Nikaidou-san’s childhood friend!”

“So she’s Kirishima-kun’s!”

“Third-year middle school, right? That uniform’s nostalgic.”

“Tall and model-like. No wonder guys hesitate to introduce her.”

“Uh, um, I…” Himeko mumbles.

From rural Tsukinose, Himeko’s unused to crowds—only chickens, sheep, or dogs at most. Older strangers overwhelm her.

(Haru-chan, help!)

She looks to Haruki, who only offers a helpless “Haha” and a wry smile.

“Your hair’s so silky! How do you care for it!?”

“That fresh sailor uniform’s cute. Our middle school had blazers; I wanna try one.”

“Lend you mine? Yup, I get Kirishima’s feelings.”

“What do you and Nikaidou-san do for fun?”

Poked and prodded, Himeko locks up under their curious stares and unfamiliar banter, emotions overwhelming, her nose stinging.

“Stop, Hime-chan’s scared! No more!” Haruki steps in.

“…Ah,” Himeko breathes.

A long, black-haired figure shields her—unfamiliar yet oddly nostalgic. Reflexively, Himeko grabs Haruki’s sweater hem. Haruki glances back with a reassuring grin, the same as Haruki from childhood.

“Hime-chan’s new here and still adjusting. Go easy, okay?” Haruki says.

“Sorry, got carried away,” one girl apologizes.

“We got too excited… Sorry?” another adds.

“I-I was just startled… I’m fine,” Himeko stammers.

Chided, the girls apologize sheepishly. Himeko accepts, flustered, and Haruki, smiling, pats her head despite their similar heights.

“Good job, Hime-chan. Well done,” Haruki teases.

“Stop, Haru-chan! I’m not a kid!” Himeko protests.

Their playful exchange, Haruki acting older and Himeko trusting despite complaints, feels warm, the girls watching fondly.

Ring—Himeko’s phone chimes.

“It’s onii. Milk, yogurt, soy sauce… Ugh, heavy stuff,” she groans.

“Haha, I’ll carry half. See you all tomorrow!” Haruki says, waving.

The girls watch them leave, murmuring.

“They seem close.”

“Never seen Nikaidou-san like that.”

“Did you hear?”

“She said ‘onii,’ right?”

“And Nikaidou-san’s stopping by her place…”

Key words spark their imaginations: reunited childhood friend, her brother, Haruki’s recent changes. Faces meet, and—

“Kya—!!” their squeals echo outside a fast-food joint.

After shopping at their usual supermarket, Himeko and Haruki walk to their apartment, shadows now equal in size.

“This is our first time walking home together, right?” Himeko says.

“Haha, different schools and all,” Haruki replies.

“Yeah, but… Is that how you are at school? Do you hang out with them a lot?”

“Nah, today was random, kinda forced. Hayato got dragged off too… Was I… weird?”

“Not weird, just… odd, knowing the real you. Like when we met at the convenience store.”

“Oh, yeah. That’s pretty much outside me.”

“So this you is our little secret, huh?”

A casual remark, yet Haruki stops, blinking.

“What’s up, Haru-chan?”

“N-Nothing! Secret… Yeah, our secret!” Haruki beams, repeating “secret” joyfully.

Himeko tilts her head, but the earlier familiarity nags, spilling out.

“I’m glad it was you, not onii, back there.”

“Huh? Why?”

“Onii’s unreliable in those moments. You’ve always protected me.”

—That’s why I liked your back.

Himeko swallows the rest, pouting. Haruki blinks, hums, and taps her chin, her cute pose far from her old tomboy self, deepening Himeko’s frown.

“No way. Hayato’s just hard to read—” Haruki starts.

“Woof! Bark! Grr… Woof!”

“!?” they gasp.

A large brown-and-white marbled dog charges, waist-high, taller if standing. A leap could be dangerous.

Why? Where’s the owner? Should we run?

Himeko’s mind spins when Haruki tosses her bag.

“Hime-chan, catch!”

“Haru-chan!?”

Haruki bolts like an arrow. Himeko sees a small girl ahead, gasping as she understands.

“Danger!” Haruki shouts.

“Fweh… Ah!?” the girl cries.

“Kyah!” Haruki yelps.

Haruki tackles the dog from behind, redirecting its attention—until it slips free, spins, and pins her down, knocking her to the ground.

“Woof! Bark! Huff… Pant!”

“M-Myah!?” Haruki squeaks.

A thud echoes. The dog looms over Haruki, who’s on her back. Himeko’s mind blanks, but Hayato’s face flashes, and she dials.

“What’s up, Himeko? Supermarket out of—”

“Help, onii-chan! Haru-chan’s being attacked and pinned, it’s bad!”

“Wait, what’s happening!?”

“Hey, Rento! Get off Nikaidou-san!” the girl shouts.

“Woof! Stop, don’t lick—Myah! Don’t eat my hair!” Haruki protests.

“Woof! Bark! Pant, woof!”

“…It’s, uh, getting drooly and bad. What do I do…?” Himeko mumbles.

“Dogs? Drool? What’s going on…?” Hayato asks.

At a small park near the supermarket, Haruki sits on a bench, slouched, as Himeko scolds her, eyeing Haruki’s sprained ankle wrapped in a wet handkerchief from the dog’s tackle.

“Seriously! You and onii are so reckless! What if you’d hurt your face!?” Himeko fumes.

“Ugh, my bad…” Haruki mumbles.

“Um, Nikaidou-san was saving me, so, uh, go easy?” the small girl interjects.

“No way! Haru-chan won’t get it without a firm word!” Himeko insists.

The girl tries to calm her. The dog, Rento, a collie, sits obediently by her, wagging its tail. A neighbor’s pet, it’s attached to her.

Up close, she’s petite and cute, her curly hair showing a distinct whorl. Her eco-bag, likely from shopping, sprouts green onion tops. But her modest green shirt strains at her chest, making Himeko’s mouth twitch. She coughs to cover.

“You okay, kid? No injuries? Good job shopping! What do kids eat to grow so… developed these days?” Himeko asks, eyes glued to her chest.

“Uh, I’m fine, but—eat, develop!?” the girl squeaks.

Himeko rubs her head vigorously, as if for luck, ignoring her squirms, her gaze serious.

“You called Haru-chan ‘Nikaidou-san.’ You know her?” Himeko asks.

“Uh, school, vegetables, gardening!” the girl stammers.

“That’s Mitake-san, my classmate,” Haruki clarifies.

“…Huh?” Himeko freezes.

Her hand stops. Turning stiffly, she meets Haruki’s exaggerated shrug and teasing grin.

“Oh? Thought Mitake-san was younger? She’s way more mature than Hime-chan in some ways~” Haruki teases.

“Shut up, Haru-chan! Sorry, Mitake-san! I’m still growing, plenty of time!” Himeko retorts.

“Sure, hope so~” Haruki smirks.

“Ugh, that smug face!” Himeko growls.

“Haha, you two are close. Nikaidou-san’s different outside school… Kinda surprising,” Minamo giggles.

“!?” they gasp.

Minamo’s laugh snaps them back, their antics a bit embarrassing.

Something nags Himeko. Haruki plays a role at school but dropped it here instantly. It clicks.

“Oh, I get it. Mitake-san’s your school friend,” Himeko says.

“Fweh!? Me, Nikaidou-san’s friend!? That’s too much!” Minamo panics, blushing.

“Not true?” Himeko asks.

Haruki, sheepish, extends a hand.

“Friends… Yeah, school and gardening friends, right!?”

“Y-Yes! School gardening friends! Um, gotta return Rento, so bye!” Minamo shakes Haruki’s hand vigorously, then drags Rento away, red-faced.

It’s over in a flash. Haruki chuckles, “Same as always.”

As Himeko stares, dumbfounded, Hayato arrives, puzzled, eyeing Haruki’s swollen ankle with an exaggerated sigh.

“Idiot, what’d you do?” he says.

“That’s harsh!” Haruki protests.

“Tch, Haruki… Here,” Hayato says, crouching with his back to her.

It’s natural. Himeko feels something settle in her chest.

“Uh, I’m too old for piggybacks…” Haruki hesitates.

“Your foot’s bad. Home okay for now?”

He lifts her despite her protests, moving as if it’s obvious—a familiar sight from Tsukinose.

When she got hurt in the mountains.

When she lost her shoes in the river.

When she napped through the day.

(…Right.)

It’s simple. As Himeko admired Haruki’s back, Haruki looked to Hayato’s.

—Always has.

A thorn in Himeko’s chest aches and slips free.

“…You’re heavier than when we were kids,” Hayato teases.

“Hey! Calling a dieting girl heavy!?” Haruki snaps, tugging his ear.

“Ow, my bad, stop!”

Himeko sees their unchanged banter.

“God, onii’s still so tactless!” she huffs.

“Right, Hime-chan, let him have it!” Haruki cheers.

“Yeah, yeah,” Hayato grumbles.

Sighing, Himeko steps ahead, leading them. Her face holds a hint of loneliness but shines clear.
