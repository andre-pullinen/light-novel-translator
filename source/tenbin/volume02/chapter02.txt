Episode 2: Inexperience
“We’re going on a diet, big bro,” Himeko said.

“Oh? Good luck… Wait, ‘we’ includes Haruki too?” Hayato replied.

“Yup. So, make us a diet-friendly dinner, please.”

“Uh, I don’t know any recipes like that.”

That was last night’s conversation.

Hayato recalls it while walking to school, picturing Himeko and Haruki. Neither seems to need a diet, but Himeko’s intense demeanor left no room for argument.

(My cooking’s mostly bar snacks and heavy stuff…)

Dieting was foreign to him. In rural Tsukinose, everyone had a car, but as a student, Hayato walked or biked everywhere. Exercise was a given, calorie counts irrelevant.

It’s a tricky issue. He grumbles that cooking duties were supposed to be shared with Himeko.

He thinks of Haruki again—slightly smaller chest but a healthy, balanced figure, likely from effort.

“Hayato, you’ve gotten good at cooking,” she’d said.

“Ugh, is this what they mean by grabbing someone’s stomach…?”

“Eating together is way better than alone.”

Her image, happily eating his food, flashes in his mind.

Maybe he is partly responsible for her weight gain.

“Might be a chance to expand my repertoire,” he says aloud, a small, eager smile forming.

Before morning homeroom, Hayato visits the school’s vegetable garden when he has time. Tending soil calms him, like in Tsukinose, and he often clears up misunderstandings with the quick-to-jump Minamo Mitake.

“Weeding? I’ll help, Mitake-san.”

“Kirishima-san!”

As expected, Minamo is diligently caring for the vegetables. Lately, it’s mostly weeding—summer brings relentless weeds.

Working together, they finish quickly. The weeds, not worth bagging, are carried to the collection site, wrapping up the morning task.

“All done?” Hayato asks.

“Yes, thank you!” Minamo replies, smiling, a smudge of dirt on her cheek.

Wearing gym clothes, a straw hat, and gloves—a far-from-stylish Tsukinose-girl look—she feels familiar, loosening Hayato’s expression.

Maybe that’s why, with his diet-menu dilemma, he casually asks, “Mitake-san, ever done a diet?”

“Wha!? Am I… fat? Should I diet!?” she panics.

“No, no! My sister! Himeko’s making me help with her diet. You don’t need it at all!”

“S-Sorry, I jumped to conclusions again… ugh…”

True to form, she overreacts but calms down, tilting her head and humming thoughtfully, eager to help. A good kid.

“Exercise and diet are key, right?” she suggests.

“I’m on food duty since I cook. But all I know are heavy, guy-food dishes or snacks.”

“Oh, you cook too, Kirishima-san? Your eggplant pickle recipe helped a lot the other day!”

“Haha, glad to hear. I learned out of necessity… my mom’s, well…”

“Was that about the hospital visit…?”

Minamo blinks, then peers at him with a curious expression—part admiration, part empathy. It shifts to a soft smile.

“You’re a great big brother, Kirishima-san.”

“W-What!? It’s not like that!” he stammers.

She giggles.

It’s Hayato’s turn to fluster. Despite her plain style, Minamo’s cute face is a diamond in the rough, enough to rattle Hayato, who’s weak to peers.

“Doing something unfamiliar for someone else is wonderful. My grandpa…” she trails off.

“Mitake-san?”

Her voice carries mixed emotions. She glances toward the hospital, her expression wistful, likely hiding something personal.

Hayato scratches his head, cursing inwardly, and faces her.

“Earlier, you said ‘you cook too.’ So, you cook?”

“Well, yeah.”

“Got any recommended recipes? Doesn’t have to be diet stuff.”

“Sure, but mine are things my grandpa likes…”

“Sounds perfect for dieting—probably low in fat.”

“Oh! …Hehe, maybe.”

They laugh, but something lingers. They’re still new acquaintances, barely knowing each other, both wanting a connection.

“I’ll send you a recipe via message—oh!” Minamo says.

“Hm? …What!?”

At the collection site behind the school—a place rarely visited outside cleaning time—they spot a tense-looking couple.

Hayato isn’t clueless. (A confession!? A confession scene!?)

His first encounter with such a moment throws him into a panic. He shouldn’t look, but he’s curious. Peeking is wrong, but moving might get him caught. His thoughts spiral.

“That guy’s Kaidou, right?” Minamo whispers.

“Kaidou…?”

“Kaidou Kazuki, super popular soccer club guy.”

“Oh…”

Prompted by her calm tone, Hayato observes. Tall, athletic, sharp eyes, and a refreshing vibe—Kaidou’s undeniably handsome, clearly a heartthrob.

Yet Minamo remains unfazed, adding to Hayato’s confusion.

As if predicting the outcome, she says flatly, “The girl will get rejected and leave soon. See—”

Sure enough, the girl’s shoulders tremble, and she flees. Kaidou stands alone, looking apologetic.

Despite witnessing an urban-legend-like confession, both Hayato and Minamo seem oddly accustomed to it. It nags at him, and in his muddled state, he asks, “…Why?”

“It’s habit. I was shocked at first, but this spot’s a confession hotspot. Not just for Kaidou.”

“Really? But you knew she’d get rejected. She was pretty cute.”

“True, that was Takakura-senpai from second year. I was a bit surprised, but there’s a rumor among girls—”

For Hayato, who’s never dated, “confession” feels like a foreign word. What follows is even harder to grasp.

“—Kaidou’s got a crush on Nikaidou-san.”

“…What?”

The words blow Hayato’s thoughts away, leaving his mind blank.

Parting with Minamo, Hayato heads to class in a daze.

(Haruki’s his crush…)

Haruki’s popular—Mori told him on his first day. Her looks convince anyone. She’s admired, relied on, and helps out often.

But since reuniting, she’s been the same old Haruki to him, even showing vulnerable sides she hides from others. She’s trouble, reckless, and impossible to ignore—that’s Haruki to Hayato.

Her popularity never felt real, and Minamo’s words seem dubious.

“Morning…” he mumbles.

“Yo, boob-maniac Kirishima,” Mori teases.

“Cut it out. What’s… that?”

In the classroom, girls huddle in serious, grim discussions. Guys act normal but seem restless.

Expecting more teasing after yesterday, Hayato’s braced, but the mood feels off.

“Listen closely, you’ll get it,” Mori says.

Puzzled, Hayato heads to his seat, catching Haruki’s group’s conversation.

“Rebounding’s the scariest.”

“Losing weight’s just the start.”

“One-food diets are total scams.”

“Hehe, intense exercise is a trap. Hunger’s the best spice, I learned that…”

“I’m losing five kilos by summer break!”

“Summer clothes show every curve…”

“This year! No exams, first-year freedom, I’ll do it!”

“Hm, so moderate exercise and watching food’s best.”

Surrounded, Haruki dives into diet talk, nodding earnestly. Some girls speak from experience, their words heating up.

With summer break nearing, talk shifts to swimsuits, fashion, wanting boyfriends, or love—making the guys antsy.

“So, dieting,” Mori says.

“My sister’s on about it too. A trend?” Hayato asks.

“Summer’s the season. Word of advice: dieting girls are moody. Treat ‘em like wounded, hungry beasts.”

“Sounds personal.”

“Haha, my girlfriend’s like that.”

“You mentioned her. Long relationship?”

“Since late middle school, not that long… but we’re childhood friends, so it’s long in a way.”

“…Huh.”

Hayato’s odd tone betrays his thoughts. To him, “childhood friend” means Haruki. She’s beautiful, her true personality known, and they’re close.

But dating her? It doesn’t click. Yet hearing Kaidou likes her stirs unease.

“…It’s confusing,” he mutters.

“Try it, maybe you’ll get it. Wanna diet with me?” Mori jokes.

“Hell no.”

“Haha, one more tip: for hungry girls, fill their hearts. Praise, pamper, touch.”

“What? To my sister? …Wait, flirting!?”

“Hahaha!”

Their half-meshed banter ends, and Hayato sits. Haruki, looking unchanged—not needing a diet—is deep in girl-talk, laughing. Once alone, playing the perfect student, this social shift is good for her.

Yet, he doesn’t want to admit it. His greeting comes out gruff.

“…Morning.”

“Good morning, Kirishima-kun,” Haruki replies.

Her radiant, perfect smile—like yesterday’s incident never happened—makes his scowl deepen.

A girl, a lively short-bobbed leader who often talks to Haruki, chimes in.

“Kirishima, still hung up on what Nikaidou-san said? Everyone knows guys like boobs, no one cares.”

“W-What!? I mean, I…” Hayato stammers.

Voices rise: “Your face is scary!” “Smile!” “I went too far yesterday, sorry.” Some defend him, others Haruki. Her apologetic eyes meet his.

“Sorry about yesterday, Kirishima-kun.”

“…Not mad,” he mutters, scratching his head and looking away.

Teasing laughter follows.

Lunch break, their secret base.

Hayato and Haruki face off, both frowning.

“Ughhh…” Haruki groans.

Her resentful stare fixes on Hayato’s bento: reheated mapo eggplant, sesame komatsuna, cherry tomatoes, broccoli, and fluffy egg with green onions—appetizing even cold.

Haruki’s lunch? A 68-kcal salad chicken bar and vegetable juice.

Eyes glued to his bento, she nibbles her bar, her stomach growling cutely. Hayato sighs heavily.

“…Want a bite?”

“No way!” she snaps.

“But, Haruki—”

“I gained four kilos!”

“…Haruki?”

Blushing, she looks down, eyes darting away.

Four kilos sounds big, but visually, it’s unnoticeable. Hayato tilts his head, frowning.

“…Doesn’t seem that different.”

No need for extreme dieting, he implies, recalling her lively diet talk with the girls. He looks away.

Haruki, as if he doesn’t get it, raises her voice.

“Hayato, you don’t understand four kilos!”

“What?”

“Listen! That’s four milk cartons, six or seven manga magazines, a medium watermelon, or a small rice bag’s worth of fat on my body!”

“O-Oh, that’s…”

Abstract before, the concrete examples hit hard. He pictures Haruki holding milk cartons, magazines, or a watermelon. No wonder Himeko freaked out.

That much fat’s hard to ignore. He gets why the girls obsess over dieting.

Haruki’s tense, her eyes flicking between his bento, his face, and elsewhere, restless. Her glances make him uneasy, deepening his frown.

“Um, uh…” she starts.

“Hm?”

“Are you… mad?”

“…What?”

Unexpected words. Her anxious, upward glance catches him off guard.

“You’ve been scowling since morning.”

“No, it’s just…”

“I went too far yesterday. Sorry…”

He thinks it’s unlike her. Normally, she’d brush it off, teasing him. But now, her eyes cling weakly.

(Moody, huh…) He recalls Mori’s advice—and the solution.

But it’s a high hurdle.

“Girls say Kaidou’s crush is Nikaidou-san,” Minamo’s words echo.

“Haruki!” he blurts.

“E-Mya!?” she squeaks.

Impulse drives him. His right hand ruffles her head, then gently strokes, scooping her hair.

Both freeze, eyes wide, speechless.

Her hair is silky, smooth, slipping through his fingers, tickling. He can’t stop stroking, entranced.

Haruki, perhaps enjoying it, relaxes, her tense eyes drooping, surrendering to his touch.

Hayato knows it’s unlike him, but his hand won’t stop. A ticklish feeling in his chest spills out.

“…Your hair.”

“Uh, yeah.”

“Forced diets can make it dry and brittle from lack of nutrients.”

“Right, I’ll be careful.”

“That’d suck.”

“W-…Yeah.”

“Take it easy. I’m rooting for you. Don’t overdo it.”

“I’ll try.”

“Man, your hair’s addictive.”

“R-Really? Hehe… I’ll keep taking care of it.”

“Oops, too much might mess it up, so—”

“—Ah.”

Regaining composure, embarrassment wins. He pulls back reluctantly.

Haruki lets out a sad sound, like a toy was taken away. Her pleading eyes ask why he’s so mean.

“Hayato…”

“H-Haruki?”

“…”

“…”

They lock eyes.

Her moist gaze shifts as she regains clarity, her face flushing with embarrassment.

“…M-Mya!”

“Haruki!?”

She shrieks, unable to hold it in.

“W-W-What was that!? That was intense, Hayato! When did you learn that!? How many girls have you charmed!?”

“Calm down! No charming! In Tsukinose, it was just Himeko, sheep, or cats!”

“You did that to Hime-chan!? That’s, like, that kind of thing, right!?”

“Your wording! Ugh, jeez! Haha!”

Her frantic “that”s try to cover her embarrassment, so very Haruki. He laughs.

She puffs her cheeks, protesting with a “Mou!”

They’re back to normal.

“Ugh, I’m not used to praise or pampering! No one’s ever petted my head!” she says, laughing awkwardly.

A shadow crosses her face, unnoticed by Hayato. He replies, slightly sulky.

“…Really? You’re popular. I thought you’d be used to confessions and stuff.”

His chest tightens. He’s curious but instantly regrets asking, frustrated with himself.

“Nope. Never been confessed to. I’ve made sure of it. I don’t share my phone ID either.”

“Really…?”

“Scandals.”

“Huh?”

“Celebrities get in trouble for dating or work issues, right?”

“Yeah…?”

“So, I, the good girl Nikaidou Haruki, can’t date anyone.”

“…Got it.”

Her sudden words confuse him. He gets the gist, but the seven-year gap blocks deeper understanding. It’s frustrating.

His face must look grim. Haruki notices, forcing a laugh to end the topic.

“Anyway, gotta diet for health!”

“For health, huh. Himeko’s trying too.”

“Yup.”

Emphasizing health, Haruki leaves much unsaid. But knowing she’s never dated or been confessed to relieves Hayato immensely.
