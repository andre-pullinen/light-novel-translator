Episode 4: Sometimes, Something Like This… Right?
Hayato, delayed by an unfamiliar bento recipe, arrives at school later than usual. The morning classroom buzzes with its typical chaos—some scramble over forgotten assignments, others beg to copy, but most chatter with friends.

“Hm?”

At his seat, his phone pings. Opening it, he finds a photo of an eggplant with a tengu-like nose, sent by Minamo Mitake. Her message reads, “Is it getting cocky?” Hayato chuckles.

More images follow: a coiled green pepper labeled “Sulking” and an upside-down one “Doing a handstand.” All are misshapen vegetables, rare in supermarkets but familiar in Tsukinose, often shared as unsellable extras.

Feeling nostalgic, Hayato’s shoulders shake at Minamo’s quirky captions.

“What’s got you grinning, Kirishima? Staring at your girlfriend?” Mori teases.

“Nope, veggies. See for yourself,” Hayato replies, showing the screen.

“Pfft, haha, what are these? Weird shapes!”

“Right? They don’t hit the market, but veggies come in all sorts.”

“Got more like this?”

“Let’s see… Oh!”

“Oh?”

A non-vegetable image flashes—Saki Mur尾’s miko photo from this morning’s recipe batch. Hayato’s startled “Oops” contrasts with Mori’s intrigued hum.

“Yup, I get it, Kirishima. Every guy loves a miko,” Mori says.

“Wait, it’s not like that! It’s for a festival, not my thing…” Hayato protests.

It’s Saki’s photo, but explaining feels tedious.

“Got it, festivals. Mikos are great, but girls in yukata at festivals, right?” Mori continues.

“Can’t deny that… Do people here wear yukata for festivals?”

“Yup, at the local summer festival. Didn’t they where you were?”

“…No young people to wear them.”

“Oh, right.”

The topic shifts to yukata, sparing Hayato further explanation. He quietly sighs in relief.

The yukata talk draws a crowd of boys.

“Yukata’s got that special vibe! Makes girls 50% cuter!”

“The hairstyles, the nape of the neck—perfection!”

“Less skin, but somehow sexier!”

“Nah, that’s too far.”

“No way.”

“C’mon, Kirishima, back me up!”

“Don’t drag me into this…”

Somehow, it’s a full-blown yukata debate. A local summer festival’s coming up, and some boys sneak glances at the girls.

Hayato glances at Haruki, catching her mischievous, curious look. He braces himself.

“Hehe, do guys really love yukata?” she asks, tilting her head, finger on her chin.

“Well, uh…”

Her teasing, calculated pose is adorable, but Hayato hears her inner voice: “Oh, boys, so into that stuff, huh?” It’s cute—to him alone.

“Of course!” a boy shouts.

“Show us your yukata, Nikaidou-san!”

“Come to the festival with us!”

“Be my girlfriend! Date me!”

Haruki’s playful jab ignites the boys, who escalate wildly. Shocked, she reels back.

“S-Sorry!?” she squeaks.

“Hey, what’re you saying!?” a girl snaps.

“Back off, you animals!”

“You okay, Nikaidou-san!?”

Girls rush to shield Haruki, who stammers, “Huh? What!?” dazed.

(Ugh, seriously…) Hayato sighs. Lately, Haruki’s clumsy side shines through. He joins the girls and Mori to calm the boys when—

“Haha, Nikaidou’s changed, huh?” Mori says.

“Dunno what you mean,” Hayato replies.

“She’s more approachable, less guarded… Maybe the girls’ rumors are true.”

“Rumors…?”

“Nikaidou Haruki’s got a boyfriend.”

“…Huh?”

Hayato’s voice sounds dumb, he knows. He barely processes half of Mori’s words.

Haruki’s changed, sure—likely for the better. But a boyfriend? It doesn’t compute.

As he grapples, the classroom entrance stirs.

“Is Nikaidou here?” a voice asks.

“Yes, what’s up?” Haruki replies.

“Speak of the devil,” Mori mutters.

“It’s him—” Hayato starts.

It’s Kazuki Kaidou, here for Haruki. Hayato checks her face—her usual perfect Nikaidou mask, not the look of someone meeting a crush. He, always by her side, knows she has no boyfriend.

Yet, unease swirls in his chest.

“Girls say Kaidou’s crush is Nikaidou-san,” Minamo’s words echo.

Glancing around, he sees girls smirking and boys watching with mixed expressions, as if the rumor’s already fact. It sours his mood.

He tries to read Kaidou’s face, but from his angle, it’s unclear. Kaidou’s animated gestures seem nervous, lending Minamo’s rumor weight.

“—So, for the club application, they told me to ask Nikaidou, a first-year…” Kaidou says.

“Right, that’s at the student council room. Shall I come with?” Haruki offers.

“That’d help, thanks.”

It’s club-related. Why Kaidou’s asking Haruki, Hayato doesn’t know. As they leave, Kaidou’s relieved glance triggers Hayato to act before thinking.

“Wait!” he shouts.

“Hya… Kirishima-kun!?” Haruki gasps.

“You are…?” Kaidou asks.

Surprised gasps ripple through the class. It’s impulsive, emotional—Hayato’s as shocked as anyone.

(Damn it!)

No taking it back. His mind races.

“Uh, you’re going for club stuff, right? I, uh, wanna join a club too, so I thought I’d tag along.”

He blurts it out, rushing past them into the hall.

“Wrong way, Kirishima-kun,” Haruki points out.

“…Ugh.”

Her correction exposes his fluster. Snickers follow, his face burning red.

“Let’s go together. That okay, Kaidou-kun?” Haruki asks.

“Yeah, I’m fine with it,” Kaidou replies, his expression complex.

Hayato, embarrassed, trails Haruki.

They walk the corridor before morning homeroom. With the bell nearing, it’s quiet, their steps quick, the mood odd—Haruki cheerful, Hayato sulky, Kaidou confused.

Kaidou breaks the silence.

“Joining a club now’s rare, huh?”

Hayato hesitates. If the rumor’s true, he’s interrupting Kaidou’s move on Haruki. But Kaidou’s tone is calm, his face genuine, not malicious.

Hayato’s reply is gruff.

“…I’m a transfer student.”

“Oh, got it. What club? Soccer’s open.”

“Gardening.”

“…Huh?”

Kaidou’s voice betrays surprise, his eyes scanning Hayato—tall, tanned, muscular arms peeking from his uniform, athletic build suggesting sports, not gardening.

Kaidou’s reaction feels like a slight against gardening, irking Hayato.

“You’re in soccer, right?” he snaps.

“Yeah, but…”

“What’s fun about it?”

“Well… the weight of a single goal, the freedom, the team’s unity… too much to list.”

“Gardening’s the same.”

Hayato stops, facing him.

“Daily watering, pruning, fertilizing, weeding, pest control—it’s hard work. But the veggies reward your effort. Each has its own personality. Odd shapes feel like a bonus. Look at this! Tilling soil’s the best!”

He thrusts his phone at them, showing Minamo’s tengu eggplant and coiled pepper.

Haruki and Kaidou blink, stunned by his passionate gardening rant—rare for a high school boy.

Haruki’s next move shocks Kaidou further.

“Haha, hahahaha! Kirishima-kun, you love gardening! That’s great, having something you’re passionate about!” she laughs, slapping his back.

“Ow! Stop hitting me!” Hayato winces.

“Nikaidou-san!?” Kaidou gasps.

Haruki, doubled over laughing, slaps Hayato’s back casually—unlike her usual school persona. Kaidou, used to her polite, kind image, gapes.

“Ahem, let’s move,” Haruki says, brushing her skirt, shifting gears.

“…Right,” Hayato mutters.

“Yeah, sure,” Kaidou agrees.

Her quick switch leaves them stunned. The student council room is a minute away.

“Excuse me—oh, no one’s here. I’ll grab the forms, wait there,” Haruki says.

“Got it,” Hayato replies.

“Thanks, Nikaidou-san,” Kaidou adds.

Flushed from earlier, Hayato scratches his head, watching Haruki rummage through cabinets with ease.

(She’s used to this.)

Seeing this unfamiliar side of her stirs a bitter feeling.

“…Didn’t know,” Kaidou murmurs.

“Hm?” Hayato responds.

“Is that the real Nikaidou Haruki?”

“…Dunno.”

Kaidou’s question feels probing, his tone certain. Hayato’s unease grows, his reply curt.

(Her facade’s supposed to stay hidden…)

It’s childish, but he resents Haruki’s earlier carelessness.

“I’m Kazuki Kaidou. You?” Kaidou asks.

“Huh?”

“Your name.”

“…Hayato Kirishima.”

Kaidou extends a hand, smiling warmly, no malice. Hayato stares, unsure of his intent.

(…)

Rumors are just rumors. Haruki’s life shouldn’t matter to him.

But he can’t help caring.

“Nice to meet you, Kirishima-kun,” Kaidou says.

“…Yeah,” Hayato replies, shaking his hand, his expression mixed.

After parting with Kaidou, Hayato returns to class with Haruki, only to be swarmed by boys.

“What the—!?” he yelps.

“Nice job, Kirishima!” one cheers.

“You stopped a hunk from stealing a beauty! You’re a hero!”

“Free for lunch and after school?”

“We gotta talk boobs!”

“And Nikaidou-san’s childhood friend stuff!”

They sling arms around him, hyped but serious, determined not to let him escape. Hayato looks for help, seeing only Mori’s shrug and Haruki mouthing, “Idiot.” Resigned, he braces for the interrogation, aware his actions drew this attention.

Haruki, watching, feels oddly elated. She knows the girls’ rumors about Kaidou’s crush. To her, he’s undeniably a catch—no flaws. But her experience tells her he’s not into her; he’s likely using the rumors, much like she does. Hayato’s interruption surprised her, and—

(Why am I… happy?)

Her own joy confuses her. Slapping his back earlier probably stemmed from this. It’s not bad, though.

Watching Hayato grilled during breaks, she can’t stop grinning. When their eyes meet, he pouts and looks away.

(Hehe.)

After being outdone by Hayato lately, her grin widens.

At lunch, a second visitor freezes Haruki’s smile.

“Is Kirishima-kun here?” Kaidou asks, smiling brightly.

“Kaidou…!?” Hayato exclaims.

He’s here for Hayato, not Haruki. The class falls silent, stunned. Even Kaidou chuckles awkwardly.

“What’s up? Why’re you here?” Hayato asks.

“To hang out. Bento for lunch? Mind if I sit?” Kaidou replies.

“Hey, hold on… ugh,” Hayato grumbles.

“Got more veggie pics like earlier?” Kaidou asks.

“Some, but…”

“Sweet, show me.”

Kaidou slides into the boys’ circle around Hayato, effortlessly natural.

“Kirishima, you had that miko pic, right?” Mori says.

“Mori, you—! Yeah, I do, but…” Hayato stammers.

“Nice, mikos are great. Festivals too. I’m into maids as well,” Kaidou adds.

Other boys and some girls jump in.

“I’m a nurse fan!”

“China dresses, yo!”

“Kaidou, you’re cool!”

“I’ve been into goth lolita…” a girl ventures.

“Me too, actually—” another adds.

Kaidou’s easygoing vibe disarms everyone, drawing them into the conversation. His charisma shines, impressing Haruki.

But she’s also confused. Seeing Hayato and Kaidou chat amiably muddles her emotions, and she doesn’t know how to handle it. The class’s stares—on her and Kaidou—intensify.

“I… gotta go,” she mutters loudly, standing.

Perfect Nikaidou having lunch plans is normal; no one questions it. Glancing at Hayato, she sees his troubled smile, which nags at her.

Leaving the classroom, Haruki heads to the secret base—a narrow, six-tatami room in the old school building.

“…Feels big,” she mumbles.

Spreading a cushion, she sits girl-style, nibbling her salad chicken bar.

(…)

It sticks in her throat, heavy in her chest. Swallowing, she checks the time—barely two minutes here, with plenty of lunch break left.

Seeing Hayato’s usual cushion, she recalls him swarmed earlier.

“…Hayato’s caught,” she sighs.

She knows it’s not true, but feels excluded, as if he’s been taken. Loneliness makes her touch her hair, recalling his strokes, and she shakes her head.

(Ugh, stop!)

Her phone pings—a message from Hayato.

“Sorry, can’t escape. You owe me one.”

Her brow furrows. Escaping that crowd’s tough, and she got stuck herself two days ago.

“Right…”

Alone, a hollow feeling hits. It’s not Hayato’s fault, but she resents him slightly.

Restless, Haruki leaves the base, wandering aimlessly. Avoiding attention limits her options, so reaching there feels inevitable.

“…Ah.”

At the garden behind the school, a petite girl in a straw hat and tracksuit—Minamo Mitake—tends vegetables. Haruki instinctively hides.

Minamo, engrossed, doesn’t notice her. They’re not close—different classes, just names known. Haruki should leave to avoid attention.

“Tilling soil’s the best!” Hayato’s words, declaring he’d join Minamo’s gardening club, echo. Her chest stirs, and her feet move toward the garden.

“Uh, hi, Mitake-san,” she says.

“Fweh, Kirish—Nikaidou-san!?” Minamo squeaks.

Her small-animal-like reaction is cute, Haruki thinks, but Minamo’s near-slip of “Kirishima” stings.

“Um, uh…” Haruki falters.

“Y-Yes, what’s up?” Minamo asks.

It’s impulsive; she has no real reason to talk. Stumbling, Haruki gets a shy smile from Minamo.

“Just… wondered what you’re doing,” Haruki says.

“P-Pruning and weeding…” Minamo replies.

“Haha, yeah, obvious, right?”

It’s plain to see, no need to ask. They share awkward smiles.

“…”

“…”

Silence falls, the air tense.

Haruki, unsure, studies Minamo. Shorter than Haruki’s 157 cm, with curly hair and a cute, petite charm.

(She’s pretty cute… Different from me.)

Could Hayato’s club choice be—

“Ugh, enough!” Haruki blurts, ruffling her hair, heedless of the mess.

“Nikaidou-san!?” Minamo gasps.

Minamo, witnessing perfect Nikaidou’s outburst, panics, fearing she’s offended her.

Seeing this, Haruki regains some calm, offering a self-mocking smile.

“Haha, sorry. I was being a jerk just now.”

“You? Um… something bothering you?” Minamo asks.

“Yeah, sorta. Lately, I guess.”

“Oh…”

Haruki sighs, exasperated with herself. Staying here might spiral her thoughts badly, so she turns to leave.

“Soil!” Minamo exclaims.

“Huh?” Haruki blinks.

“Let’s till soil! It’s great, calms you! Here!” Minamo thrusts her straw hat onto Haruki, dragging her along—surprisingly forceful for her shy look.

At the eggplant with purple flowers, Minamo smiles, offering pruning shears.

“Cut these extra branches—snip ‘em off!” she says.

“Uh, this’ll take out a third. Sure?” Haruki asks.

“Yup, go bold! It’s like a haircut for veggies.”

“Okay, here goes!”

Guided, Haruki prunes. Each vegetable is unique, no standard approach. It’s puzzling but engaging. Sweating, her cheeks dirt-smudged like Minamo’s, she dives in.

“Here, just slice like whoosh? Too much?” she asks.

“They’re tough, no worries,” Minamo assures.

“Alright! Hehe, it’s weird thinking these flowers become veggies.”

“Amazing, right?”

“Oh, next harvest, can I—”

“Come back!” Minamo beams, then pauses. “Nikaidou-san?”

Haruki, caught up, meets Minamo’s kind gaze and realizes her excitement. Her face heats.

“Uh, I…” she stammers.

“Hehe, you smiled,” Minamo says.

“Ugh…” Haruki groans.

Minamo sees through her, embarrassing her. Moving beside Haruki, Minamo focuses on the plants.

“A while back, something sad happened. I cried, but my stomach still growled, which was funny. Flowers end when they bloom, but veggies start there, becoming fruit. That’s why I started, out of curiosity,” Minamo says.

“Mitake-san…?” Haruki murmurs.

“It was all new. Caring for them’s harder than I thought, but so fun… Ugh, what am I saying? I mean, doing what I love saved me!” Minamo blushes, gripping her hands, earnest.

Haruki stares. It’s just encouragement—Minamo’s way of cheering her up. Her eyes hold no ulterior motives; Haruki can tell.

(She’s a good person.)

Minamo’s pure kindness shifts Haruki’s view of her, sparking admiration and curiosity.

“Can I come back here?” Haruki asks.

“Yes, please!” Minamo beams.

Her radiant smile dazzles Haruki.

After school, post-homeroom, Hayato’s still mobbed by Mori and the boys.

“Yo, Kirishima, there’s a big shrine a train ride away,” one says.

“Gotta see summer mikos in light clothes!” another adds.

“How about a maid café instead?”

“Wait, maid cafés are real…?” Hayato marvels.

It’s an invitation to hang out. Stunned by “maid café,” he glances at Haruki for help.

Haruki nearly teases, “Into maids, huh?” but swallows it. Joining this guy-talk risks repeating the yukata fiasco. She forces a dry laugh, her brow furrowing, chest uneasy.

As girls approach her and Mori grabs Hayato, Haruki steps between them.

“Hey, Nikaidou-san, wanna—” a girl starts.

“Time for Kirishima’s welcome party at a maid—” Mori begins.

“Kirishima-kun, uh, that thing needs fixing, can you help?” Haruki interrupts.

Her vague words silence the room, all eyes on her. It’s impulsive, driven by her unease, but she can’t explain it.

Confused, she freezes, but Hayato picks up the cue.

“That? The club form from this morning? Something wrong?” he asks.

“Yes! The advisor’s leaving soon, so we need to fix it,” she says.

“Gotta hurry then. Sorry, Mori, next time!” Hayato says, escaping.

“Wait, Kirishima-kun!” a girl calls.

Hayato bolts, Haruki following. The classroom erupts behind them, startling her. They share a wry smile and race to the exit.

“Haruki, your moves are so predictable. Same as transfer day,” Hayato teases.

“Shut up! I wanna stop somewhere, come with,” she retorts.

“Sure thing.”

Despite late afternoon, summer sun blazes high.

Haruki heads home.

“Your place? Something up?” Hayato asks.

“Dunno… just felt like it?” she replies.

“Haruki?”

His voice carries surprise. Honestly, there’s no real reason. Maybe dieting’s fogging her brain.

She voices her gut feeling.

“Um, I kinda wanna play that action RPG together again.”

“Oh, right, you only play it with me,” he says.

“Haha, yup.”

Smirking, they kick off shoes and enter her room. Haruki cranks the AC, tossing her socks. Hayato slumps onto a cushion, sighing, “Haaah.”

“Hayato, you sound like an old man,” she teases.

“Says you!” he shoots back.

“Pfft.”

Haruki fans her blouse, sprawling lazily. As she starts the console, Hayato murmurs, “Sometimes, this is nice.”

“!?”

Her heart skips, but warmth spreads. He’s scratching his head, embarrassed.

(…)

Lately, she’s been at Hayato’s for dinner, making her place a rare stop. The relaxed vibe, unlike school or his house, brings a childlike grin as she hands him a controller.

“Yeah, here,” she says.

“Cool, but I gotta get back before Himeko starves and riots,” he jokes.

“Haha, got it.”

They exchange mischievous smiles, memories flashing—finding a field of morning glories, a riverbed rich with flat stones, or building a secret base in an abandoned shrine. It’s the same grin from sharing those secrets.

The summer sun shines as brightly as it did then.
