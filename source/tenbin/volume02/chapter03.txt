Episode 3: An Unexpected Gift
At the Kirishima family’s dinner table, just before sunset, two girls voice their complaints.

“Big bro, this is awful. The presentation’s just bad,” Himeko says.

“Even I… yeah, this is rough,” Haruki adds.

“…I know. Even I think it’s a bit much,” Hayato admits.

Spread on the table: salted edamame, sliced chilled tomatoes, spicy miso-dressed cucumber sticks, and the main dish—chicken skin with ponzu, onions, and green onions. Instead of rice, cold tofu.

It’s practically a perfect bar menu, screaming for a beer.

“I forgot… your cooking’s all bar-snack stuff,” Himeko groans.

“Haha… yeah, Hayato, uh, haha…” Haruki laughs awkwardly.

“Look, it’s not pretty, but it’s diet-friendly, right?”

Despite the grumbling, Haruki and Himeko clean their plates. The taste, at least, isn’t an issue.

Hayato had researched diet-friendly options: high protein, low calorie, low carb, and fiber-rich. This menu, chosen from his repertoire, earned the “awful presentation” critique.

Back in his room, Hayato flops onto his bed, mulling it over.

“Hmm, diet menus…”

He wants them to enjoy his cooking—that’s his honest feeling. But no great ideas come.

(Maybe ask for advice…)

He messages Minamo Mitake, attaching a photo of dinner with a note: “Not a hit.”

Ten minutes later, a reply comes with a wry smile emoji, her confusion clear. He sends another message.

“Sorry for the weird pic. I thought veggies were key for diets… Any ideas? Don’t want Himeko complaining.”

“Hmm… summer veggie tempura soaked in broth is tasty, but…”

“That’s deep-fried then soaked in dashi? Hmm…”

“Too high in calories. Sorry, I’m not much help.”

“No, sounds tasty. Mind sharing the recipe?”

“Sure, I’ll send it later.”

“Thanks.”

After the exchange, he sits up, scratching his head. Checking his contacts, he sees Mori and a few male classmates—none likely to offer diet-menu advice.

(I don’t know many girls… Oh, wait.)

He heads to Himeko’s room across the hall.

“Himeko, got a sec?”

“! W-What!? Don’t just barge in!” she snaps.

“Whoops, sorry.”

Opening the door without waiting, he finds Himeko cross-legged, straining in a stretch, a magazine open before her. Clearly embarrassed, she growls, demanding he state his business.

“Can you give me Mur尾’s ID?” he asks.

“Huh? Saki-chan’s?”

Surprised, Himeko frowns, eyeing him suspiciously. After a sigh, she speaks.

“Big bro, hitting on my friends just ‘cause you’re not popular is kinda low.”

“What!? No way! I just… wanted to ask something and needed her contact!”

“Sure. But she’s basically scared of you.”

“Ugh, yeah, you’re right…”

Himeko’s best friend, Saki Mur尾, and Hayato have an awkward history. She hides behind Himeko or flees when they meet alone. Himeko’s grilled him over pranks, but there’s none.

Seeing him slump, Himeko sighs again.

“Fine, I’ll pass your ID to Saki-chan. Whether she replies is up to her.”

“…Thanks.”

Shooed out, Hayato leaves. Thinking he’s failed to connect with Saki, he sets his phone aside, does chores, and heads to the bath.

A drip hits the tiles.

“…What to do,” Hayato sighs, soaking in the tub, staring at the ceiling.

Unlike their Tsukinose home, this apartment bathroom has no window. His sigh melts into the air.

He thinks of Saki, Himeko’s friend. Her youthful, refined face, pale skin, and flaxen hair give her a mystical aura, fitting for the daughter of a shrine family with Heian roots. Gentle and always smiling, she’s the opposite of lively Himeko.

In Tsukinose, the elderly doted on her like a granddaughter, making her a local idol.

But she’s distant with Hayato.

(Well, I was the only guy her age in Tsukinose…)

He’s unsure how to approach her, a rare peer. As Himeko’s friend and with her sacred kagura dance holding special meaning for him, he thought messaging might work.

(Himeko’s right, though…)

Girls are tricky, he thinks. Haruki’s mischievous grin flashes in his mind, teasing, “Idiot.” Frowning, he sinks half his face into the water, blowing a big bubble.

Back in his room, time has passed.

“…Hm?”

His phone shows notifications from an unknown address. The subject reads, “From Saki Mur尾.” The message: “What’s up?”— curt but prompt, sent an hour ago, right after he left Himeko’s room.

(Crap…)

Feeling bad for the delay, he carefully types a reply.

“Been a while, right? Sorry for the sudden ask, but Himeko started dieting last night. Know any good meal ideas? Thought you might know, being a girl and all.”

Saki’s reply is quick, despite the wait. Her typing speed impresses him.

“I see.”

“I’ve never dieted, so I wouldn’t know.”

“Maybe search ‘diet meal recommendations’?”

Curt, as expected from their Tsukinose days. But her duty to Himeko likely prompted the reply. He smiles wryly.

“Thanks.”

“That’s it?”

“Let me know if you think of anything.”

“Sure.”

The exchange ends. Her tone’s unchanged, but getting a reply is a win. He stretches.

“Might as well research before bed.”

Following Saki’s advice, he searches but finds only convenience store tips, chain restaurant calorie counts, and nutritional breakdowns. Distracted, night deepens, and he drifts off.

“…Huh, fell asleep?”

Morning light fills the room. The clock shows his usual wake-up time.

Blame the phone—unlike a PC, it’s too easy to browse in bed, leading to a sleep crash.

“Ow, my neck… Hm?”

New messages await.

“Shirattaki pasta, chicken breast dishes, mushroom-packed omurice… Wow, but…”

All from Saki. Detailed, clear recipes—visually appealing enough for Haruki and Himeko to like. Impressed, he notes they were sent late at night, post-midnight.

(She researched for Himeko, huh… Wait, what’s this!?)

Among the images, one isn’t food.

“This year’s festival outfit. What do you think?”

Attached is Saki in her festival miko attire: traditional white robe, scarlet hakama, a sheer chihaya with gold embroidery, plus hair accessories and makeup. Her ethereal beauty, already striking with her pale features, is elevated to otherworldly.

Her smile, natural and unlike any Hayato’s seen, captivates him. Despite knowing she’s Himeko’s friend, he swallows hard.

(This is… No, no, hold on.)

Why she sent it, he doesn’t know. But he recalls Himeko asking his opinion on new clothes. This must be similar—no room for misinterpretation.

Clearing his throat, he types a reply, driven by gratitude.

“Thanks, Himeko’ll love these. The outfit looks great, as always. Cute.”

No immediate reply. It’s early, after all.

Stretching, he starts his morning routine. The cloudless sky outside promises another hot day. He groans, “Ugh…”

Saki’s reply arrives over an hour later, en route to school.

“I see.”

As curt as ever, Hayato gives a wry smile.
